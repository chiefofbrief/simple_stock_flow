## A. Third-party article excerpts

### A1 — CoWoS / advanced packaging is the real chokepoint

> Every chip Nvidia ships, every custom AI accelerator Google, Amazon, Microsoft, and Meta run in their data centers, all of it starts at one company. That part you probably know. What you likely don't know is that TSMC's real stranglehold on the AI economy isn't in its fabrication fabs. It's in a separate process that happens after the chip is made. A step called advanced packaging. A technology called CoWoS…. The semiconductor supply chain is usually described in one beat: design the chip, fabricate it at TSMC, ship it. That's incomplete.

> When TSMC completes a 3nm silicon wafer, it is not a finished chip yet. The wafer is only filled with many tiny chip pieces called dies, which still need to be cut, packaged, and tested before they can be used in products… Before any AI accelerator can train a model or run inference, those dies need to be bonded together with high-bandwidth memory (HBM) through an ultra-precise process… for the specific requirements of modern AI chips, the only version that works at scale is TSMC's CoWoS (Chip-on-Wafer-on-Substrate.)… Advanced packaging prices are rising at 2-4x the rate of the wafers themselves. Scarcity isn't in the silicon, it's in the glue. And that gap is compounding in TSMC's margins every quarter….

> At the end of 2024, TSMC's CoWoS capacity sat at approximately 35,000 wafers per month. By the end of 2025, it had scaled to roughly 75,000. Its target for the end of 2026 is around 125,000 to 130,000 wafers per month, a near-4x expansion in under two years. It is still not enough to meet demand…

> Broadcom carries a $73 billion AI chip backlog and is targeting $100 billion in annual AI chip revenue by 2027. In Q1 FY2026, Broadcom's AI semiconductor revenue hit $8.4 billion, a 106% year-over-year jump with guidance of $10.7 billion in Q2. But Broadcom doesn't own a single fab. Every chip it designs for Google, Meta, and OpenAI gets fabricated and packaged at TSMC. Broadcom's revenue conversion rate is, at a structural level, a function of what TSMC schedules on its CoWoS line.

### A2 — The custom-ASIC wave routes through TSMC

> One of the quieter shifts in AI hardware over the past two years is the move by every major cloud company to build its own chips. Google has the TPU v7 Ironwood. Amazon has Trainium 3. Microsoft has Maia 200. Meta has MTIA. OpenAI is building its custom chip through a $10 billion partnership with Broadcom. This entire custom silicon wave is being enabled almost entirely by TSMC. Broadcom and Marvell together control roughly 95% of the ASIC… co-design market. Every chip they design routes through TSMC for fabrication, and then through TSMC's CoWoS line for packaging. Custom ASICs are growing at approximately 44% CAGR, according to TrendForce, against roughly 16% for standard GPUs. That means demand on TSMC's packaging line is accelerating from multiple directions at the same time: Nvidia needs slots for its own GPU products, and all five hyperscalers need slots for their proprietary ASIC programs.

### A3 — The packaging revenue engine that's not in analyst models

> If CoWoS capacity and ASP rise as expected, TSMC's packaging business could nearly double into a $6-7B quarterly revenue engine by 2027, even without adding logic wafer output. That projection exists nowhere in published analyst models, which still treat packaging as a footnote to TSMC's wafer story.

> A useful historical comparison: when OPEC tightened oil supply in the 1970s, the real power wasn't just in owning the oil. It was in controlling the pipelines and terminals through which oil moved globally. The infrastructure constraint became the pricing lever. TSMC's CoWoS position functions similarly. The world needs the AI equivalent of refined oil. TSMC controls almost every refinery capable of producing it at scale.

### A4 — Threats to the packaging position

> Packaging monopolies don't last indefinitely. Alternatives are emerging. Alternative packaging could pressure margins: Powertech's PiFO, which uses glass substrates, is reportedly around 30% cheaper than TSMC's CoWoS. If it scales commercially before 2027, it could weaken TSMC's pricing power in advanced packaging.
> Outsourcing helps, but does not fix the bottleneck: TSMC is reportedly sending 240,000 to 270,000 wafers annually to partners like Amkor and SPIL to ease capacity pressure. But its Arizona advanced packaging facility is not expected to reach volume production before 2028, keeping supply tight until then.

> When CEO C.C. Wei said at this year's shareholder meeting that the AI chip shortage will "persist for years," he wasn't talking about wafer capacity. He was talking about packaging. The bottleneck is the glue. And the company that controls the glue determines when every major AI chip ships, who gets priority, and what everything downstream costs.

### A5 — Pricing philosophy (cost-first, deliberately under-pricing)

> Apart from also being on an Island, TSMC, like me, is a cost-first organisation. Even though they have a unique position in the semiconductor supply network, they are not using or abusing it to crank up prices like a US corporation would feel obliged to do. I am not saying that TSMC is not increasing pricing. I am saying it is not their primary focus. They are following the best sales advice I ever got: Never disturb a customer who is busy buying. Rather than using the leverage directly as Nvidia or Intel would, TSMC pursues a long-term plan to embed the client more deeply into its ecosystem. The benefits of a new node are given to the clients up front, while TSMC has to drag the profitability out of the red and above the corporate point: The average gross margin of the company. Despite being the most successful node ever, the 3nm process has not reached the corporate point yet, which will happen sometime during 2026. The new 2nm node and the overseas expansions will drag down TSMC's margins over the years to come, and while they pass some of the bill to the customers, it is nowhere near what it could have been.

### B2 — Revenue recognition (2025 annual)

> The Company recognizes revenue when performance obligations are satisfied. The performance obligations are satisfied when customers obtain control of the promised goods, which is generally when the goods are delivered to the customers' specified locations… Revenue is reduced for estimated customer returns, rebates and other similar allowances… payment term granted to customers is due 30 days from the invoice date or 15 days from the end of the month of when the invoice is issued.

### B3 — Inventory valuation (2025 annual)

> Inventories are stated at the lower of cost or net realizable value… The Company estimates the net realizable value of inventory for normal waste, obsolescence and unmarketable items at the end of reporting period and then writes down the cost of inventories to net realizable value. The net realizable value of the inventory is determined mainly based on assumptions of future demand within a specific time horizon.

### B4 — Q1 2026 revenue analysis (management report)

> In the first quarter, revenue increased 8.4% quarter-over-quarter… By technology, 3nm process technology contributed 25% of total wafer revenue in 1Q26 while 5nm and 7nm accounted for 36% and 13% respectively. Advanced technologies (7nm and below) accounted for 74% of total wafer revenue. By platform, HPC and Smartphone represented 61% and 26% of net revenue respectively… Sequentially, revenue from HPC, IoT, DCE and Others increased 20%, 12%, 28%, and 17% respectively. Smartphone and Automotive decreased 11% and 7%… revenue from customers based in North America accounted for 76% of total net revenue in 1Q26…

### B5 — Chairman's letter / AI conviction (2025 annual)

> 2025 was another strong year for TSMC… the Foundry 2.0 industry increased 16% year-over-year… TSMC's revenue increased 35.9% year-over-year in US dollar terms, and both our revenue and EPS set new record highs… The explosive growth in token volume demonstrates increasing consumer AI model adoption. Enterprise AI is another source of demand… we continue to observe the rising emergence of Sovereign AI. Thus, our conviction in the AI megatrend is strengthening… In its third full year of volume ramp, 3-nanometer technologies represented 24% of our total wafer revenue in 2025. Our 2-nanometer technology successfully entered high volume manufacturing in 4Q'25, with good yield, and we expect a fast ramp in 2026.

### B6 — 3DFabric / advanced-packaging roadmap (2025 annual, condensed)

> CoWoS® advanced packaging service integrates multiple SoC chips and HBM stacks… CoWoS®-S (silicon interposer) in volume production for several years; CoWoS®-R entered its third year of volume production in 2025; CoWoS®-L entered its second year of volume production in 2025, adopted by multiple products with larger reticle interposer size expected to start volume production in 2026. TSMC-SoIC® 3nm CoW stacking entered volume production in 2025. TSMC-SoW™ (system-on-wafer) first-gen (logic only) in second year of volume production in 2025; second gen (logic + HBM) in development. CoWoS®-L at 3.5-reticle in production since 2024; 5.5-reticle CoWoS®-L to finish qualification in 2026; 9.5-reticle CoWoS®-L in progress. CoWoS® Co-packaged optics (CPO) under development. TSMC-COUPE™ silicon photonics volume production expected 2026.

### B7 — Capital deployment (2025 annual + Q1 2026)

> R&D total expenditures amounted to 6.5% of revenue in 2025… TSMC Arizona's first facility began volume production of 4nm in Q4 2024; second facility completed, installing systems for 3nm and more advanced; third facility began construction in 2025. TSMC announced plans for three additional fabs, two advanced packaging facilities and a major R&D center in the US; specialty fab in Dresden, Germany, began construction in 2024. Capital expenditures totaled US$11.10 billion in 1Q26.

### B8 — Q1 2026 financial highlights + bottleneck signals

> Net revenue NT$1,134.10 billion (+8.4% QoQ, +35.1% YoY). Gross margin 66.2% (+3.9pp QoQ, +7.4pp YoY). Operating margin 58.1% (+4.1pp QoQ, +9.6pp YoY). Net income NT$572.48 billion (+13.2% QoQ, +58.3% YoY). Net margin 50.5%; diluted EPS NT$22.08.
