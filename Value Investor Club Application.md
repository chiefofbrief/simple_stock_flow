## Description

Intuit trades at 15.7x owner earnings following a 65% P/E compression driven by fears that AI will displace TurboTax. Those fears are contradicted by the company's own financial results: the AI-enabled version of TurboTax now represents 53% of TurboTax revenue and grew 38% this year. With the bear scenario already priced in and a specific catalyst three months out, this is a dollar for 70 cents.

### The Setup

On May 21, 2026, Intuit reported Q3 FY2026 results: EPS of $12.80 against an estimate of $12.48, revenue of $8.56B against $8.52B, and a raise to full-year guidance of 13-14% revenue growth and +18% non-GAAP EPS growth. Simultaneously, management announced a 17% workforce reduction, approximately 3,200 employees, with $300-340M in restructuring charges. The stock fell roughly 20% intraday to as low as $305, its lowest level since September 2020 and its largest single-day decline since 2003. It has since stabilized near $320.

The result: a business now trading at 15.7x owner earnings (free cash flow minus stock-based compensation), having compounded EPS at 19.4% annually for five years, generating $7.71B in free cash flow at a 36.8% FCF margin, with Debt/OCF of 0.87x and ROIC of 21.3%, back above the 20% threshold that confirms a durable competitive advantage. Nine Wall Street firms maintained Buy or Outperform ratings on the day of the drop. At $320, the stock sits below every price target in a 34-analyst coverage universe, including the most bearish at $360.

### The Market Narrative and Where It Goes Wrong

The bear case is identifiable and widely held: AI agents will disintermediate TurboTax, eroding unit volume and pricing power. IRS Direct File's permanent expansion adds a government-backed free alternative. The narrative has factual grounding — TurboTax did lose federal units this season. The market has responded by compressing Intuit's P/E from 38.4x twelve months ago to approximately 14x non-GAAP today, a 65% multiple reduction over a period when TTM EPS grew from $19.46 to $23.04. The 1-year price/earnings correlation is -0.96: as earnings rose 18.4%, price fell roughly 57%. That is the signature of a sentiment-driven dislocation.

The error in the bear case lies in what it is actually measuring. The customers Intuit lost this season are, per management's Q3 call characterization, "the most price-sensitive DIY filers earning less than $50,000 annually." Management's exact words: "We lost on price." IRS Direct File is free. This is a price argument, not a product quality or relevance argument. The segment that exited is the lowest-ARPU, highest-churn, lowest-margin tier of the franchise. The business did not lose its best customers. It lost the ones it charges the least.

Here is the critical inversion the market is missing. Intuit's own AI-driven product, TurboTax Live, combines AI tools with a credentialed human expert network and now represents 53% of total TurboTax revenue, up 11 percentage points in a single year. TurboTax Live customers grew 38% in FY2026. Revenue from the product grew 36%. The product the AI disruption narrative is supposed to threaten is the company's fastest-growing, highest-ARPU segment. The disruption narrative is accurate about where competition is entering, the free and low-price DIY tier, and wrong about what that means for the franchise. Intuit is not being replaced by AI; it is migrating its customer base toward the AI-plus-human-expert combination at a pace that more than offsets the low-end exits in revenue terms. TurboTax total revenue grew 7% despite the unit loss, because ARPU expanded 11%.

The same logic holds in small business. QuickBooks Online Accounting grew 22% in Q3 at 77% operating margins. Replacing QuickBooks requires migrating multi-year accounting records, payroll integrations, payments infrastructure, and embedded accountant relationships. No competitor has demonstrated the ability to replicate that combination at the SMB level. There is no AI disruption signal anywhere in QBO's financial data.

### The Financials

Over five years, Intuit's EPS has compounded at 19.4% annually, from $9.50 to $23.04, while the stock has produced a negative price return over the same period. The entire story is P/E derating: from a 5-year average of 44.7x down to 15.7x owner earnings today. At no point did the company fail to generate profit across those five years, miss FCF expectations structurally, or show impaired competitive position in its core segments.

Owner earnings, calculated as FCF of $7.71B minus SBC of $2.04B, stand at $5.67B against a market cap of $89B, a multiple of 15.7x. This is the correct economic anchor. The non-GAAP P/E of approximately 14x excludes $2.04B in annual stock-based compensation that is a genuine recurring labor cost. At 15.7x owner earnings on a business compounding those earnings at 15-18% annually, the price embeds near-permanent deceleration that the last three quarters of reported results do not support.

FCF of $7.71B represents a 36.8% margin on $20.93B in revenue. Capital expenditure runs at 0.8% of revenue, making this a near-zero-capex business where FCF grows nearly 1:1 with operating income. ROIC of 21.3% TTM, recovered from a 10.1% post-acquisition trough in 2022 through five consecutive years of expansion, confirms the competitive moat is intact and still widening. Debt/OCF of 0.87x means all debt could be retired in under a year from operating cash flows. The company repurchased over $2.4B of its own stock in the first nine months of FY2026, up more than 60% year over year.

The restructuring is being misread as distress. A company with $6.8B in cash, $7.71B in annual free cash flow, and a raised full-year guidance issued on the same day as the cut is not reducing headcount out of necessity. It is reshaping the cost base ahead of a new monetization model: management announced a consumption-based pricing structure for AI and human expert services on the Q3 call, with confirmed early adoption in the highest-value customer tiers on QBO Advanced and Intuit Enterprise Suite. That model has no financial contribution yet and is not embedded in current valuation.

### The Bear Case

The legitimate risk is ARPU saturation. Intuit is trading unit volume for ARPU expansion, and that trade works only as long as the remaining customer base continues migrating upmarket. TurboTax ARPU grew 11% on 2% unit growth this season. The structural question is what happens when the population willing to upgrade from DIY to TurboTax Live is exhausted. If ARPU growth decelerates to 3-5% while units continue declining, TurboTax revenue turns negative. GBS and Credit Karma are not sized to compensate for a Consumer segment in outright decline.

This scenario has a specific observable trigger: TurboTax revenue below 0% growth in the FY2027 tax season alongside QBO paying customer count declining. Neither signal is present in current data.

## Catalyst

Under the bear scenario, flat owner earnings at a 13-15x multiple implies a business worth approximately $265-305 per share, just below current. The downside is bounded by how cheaply the stock already trades. In the base case, with owner earnings growing 15-18% in FY2027 as restructuring savings materialize and GBS maintains its 15-16% trajectory, and a re-rating to 20-22x as operating leverage demonstrates, the stock is worth approximately $470-530, representing roughly 47-66% upside from $320.

The primary catalyst is Q4 FY2026 earnings on August 20, 2026. This is not a routine report. It will be the first quantification of annualized restructuring savings, a figure management has not disclosed and the single most important unresolved variable for the FY2027 EPS thesis. It will also produce the first FY2027 revenue and EPS guidance. Management committed explicitly on the Q3 call to "at least mid-teens annual EPS growth over the coming years." August 20 is where that commitment meets hard numbers.

At $320, the stock sits below every analyst price target across 34-firm coverage. Nine firms maintained Buy or Outperform on the day of the 20% drop. Institutional ownership stands at 83.7%. A beat-and-raise producing the largest single-day decline in 23 years, on a business at 15.7x owner earnings that has compounded EPS at 19% annually for five years, with the bear scenario already reflected in the price: this is a dollar for 70 cents.
