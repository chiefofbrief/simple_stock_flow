# AI Supply Chain — Company Index

*Last updated: 2026-05-10*

Company-centric companion to `context_ai_supply_chain.md`. Purpose: fast, focused snapshot of every publicly traded company in the AI supply chain for use during stock analysis and tracker population. Each entry covers role, tier, competitive position, nearest alternatives, and key risks.

**How this file is used:**
- **Stock analysis (Steps 1/Pass 1/Pass 2):** Load by default for any `AI SC` ticker. Provides supply chain context for that specific company without loading the full encyclopedia.
- **Tracker population:** Use the Quick Reference Table to identify layer gaps and uninvestigated companies.
- **Not for:** Deep thematic or cross-cutting research — load `context_ai_supply_chain.md` for that.

---

## Tier Definitions

| Tier | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **IRREPLACEABLE** | Monopoly or near-monopoly in a supply-chain-gating function. No credible near-term alternative exists. Pricing power is structural. If this company is constrained, downstream supply breaks. |
| **CRITICAL** | Dominant position; alternatives exist but are 2–4+ years from parity or materially inferior. Structural pricing power; moat is durable but not absolute. |
| **LEVERAGED** | Meaningful AI tailwind with real revenue impact, but operates in competitive markets, has near-term alternatives, or is cyclically sensitive. Strong thesis but requires more selective entry. |

## IRREPLACEABLE

### ASML — ASML Holding
**Layer(s):** L2  
**Tier:** IRREPLACEABLE  
**Role:** Sole manufacturer of EUV lithography machines — the only tools capable of printing circuit patterns at leading-edge nodes (7nm and below). Every advanced AI chip produced anywhere in the world is physically impossible without ASML's EUV scanners. Also supplies DUV lithography for mature nodes. Next-generation High-NA EUV extends the monopoly into the next process node cycle.  
**Competitive Position:** Absolute monopoly on EUV, built over decades with hundreds of irreplaceable supplier relationships globally. No competitor has produced a commercially viable EUV scanner. Canon and Nikon produce DUV tools but cannot access leading-edge nodes.  
**Nearest Alternatives:** None.  
**Key Risks:** Export controls limiting China revenue (known and partially priced). Single-point-of-failure systemic risk for global chip supply.

### HXSCL — SK Hynix
**Layer(s):** L5  
**Tier:** IRREPLACEABLE  
**Role:** Primary supplier of HBM3E to Nvidia and Google — the highest-bandwidth memory stacked directly on AI accelerators. Technology leader in HBM packaging (TC-NCF → MR-MUF → hybrid bonding roadmap). Completed validation of 12-high HBM using hybrid bonding; ordered first mass-production-intent hybrid bonding equipment (Applied Materials/Besi joint system, ~$15M). Q1 2026: 72% operating margin, fourth consecutive record quarter, KRW 52.5T revenue. Transitioning to LTA-only model with Microsoft and Google: Microsoft 3-year DDR5 LTA (value in "tens of billions of dollars"), Google 5-year general-purpose DRAM contract with 2-year HBM extension option — both include 10%–30% prepayments (up from sub-5% historically) with full prepayment forfeiture if purchase volumes are not met. Also co-developing High Bandwidth Flash (HBF) with SanDisk — a new memory tier delivering 1.6 TB/s read bandwidth at NAND-like cost, targeting AI inference workloads; samples H2 2026.  
**Competitive Position:** 2+ year technology lead over Samsung on HBM yields. HBM demand over the next 3 years already exceeds SK Hynix's available supply — they explicitly cannot accommodate all customer requests. DRAM accounted for 78% of Q1 revenue; HBM customer demand for 3 years exceeds capacity.  
**Nearest Alternatives:** Samsung (materially behind on yields, 2–3 years to parity). Micron (ramping HBM3E on a cost-differentiated approach, not yet performance-parity).  
**Key Risks:** Korean-listed (OTC via HXSCL) — US liquidity constraints. Samsung closing the yield gap is the primary thesis risk. Rubin delays create short-term demand timing risk.

### KLAC — KLA Corporation
**Layer(s):** L2  
**Tier:** IRREPLACEABLE  
**Role:** Near-monopoly in process control and metrology — the inspection and measurement tools that verify chip manufacturing quality at every fabrication step. Without process control, yields collapse and advanced fabs cannot operate economically. Every wafer at every leading-edge fab requires KLA tools. High-margin service and recurring revenue from installed base.  
**Competitive Position:** Near-monopoly in its core metrology sub-category. Revenue lags foundry CapEx by 12–18 months, providing order book visibility. TSMC CapEx at high end of $52–56B range is the primary leading indicator.  
**Nearest Alternatives:** Applied Materials and ASML offer limited metrology capabilities; none match KLA's specialization or market share in process control.  
**Key Risks:** Revenue tied to foundry CapEx cycles with a lag. Export controls cap China TAM.

### TSM — Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing
**Layer(s):** L3  
**Tier:** IRREPLACEABLE  
**Role:** The sole manufacturer capable of producing the world's most advanced AI silicon at scale. Every major AI accelerator converged on TSMC N3 simultaneously (NVDA Rubin, AMD MI400, Google TPU v8, AWS Trainium3, Meta MTIA) alongside Apple A19/A19 Pro and M-series. N3 utilization on track to exceed 100% in H2 2026. Apple CEO Tim Cook confirmed in Q2 FY2026 earnings (April 2026) that TSMC 3nm is Apple's primary production bottleneck — the first major consumer-side confirmation of N3 scarcity operating simultaneously with AI accelerator demand. CoWoS ASP ~$10,000/wafer (parity with 7nm logic), advanced packaging 10% of revenue. Q2 2026 guidance: $39.0–40.2B (+32% YoY), gross margins 65.5–67.5%. 3nm expanding to 180K wafers/month by end 2026 (20% above original target); 2nm ramping to ~100K wafers/month by end 2026.  
**Competitive Position:** Process lead approximately 2–3 years over Samsung, 4+ years over Intel. Ecosystem lock-in via process design kits. Deliberately not pricing to scarcity — any pricing philosophy shift would be immediately and massively accretive.  
**Nearest Alternatives:** Samsung 2nm (mid-50% yields vs. TSMC's 80–90%). Intel 18A (sub-50% yields, 2027+ story). No credible alternative at scale today.  
**Key Risks:** Taiwan geopolitical risk is the single largest systemic risk in the entire AI supply chain. N2/A16 overseas fab buildout dilutes gross margins 2–3%.

## CRITICAL

### AMAT — Applied Materials
**Layer(s):** L2, L5  
**Tier:** CRITICAL  
**Role:** Broadest equipment portfolio across deposition, etch, and inspection — tools required across every node type and every chip category. Co-developed the hybrid bonding inline system with Besi that SK Hynix ordered (~$15M) for HBM4 mass production — a new incremental revenue vector in advanced memory packaging. TSMC CapEx at high end of $52–56B range provides multi-year order visibility.  
**Competitive Position:** Most diversified equipment revenue across node types — no single customer or process dominates. Breadth is the defense against single-layer disruption.  
**Nearest Alternatives:** LRCX (etch/deposition overlap), ASML (lithography — different sub-layer), Tokyo Electron.  
**Key Risks:** Cyclical revenue tied to foundry CapEx. China revenue ceiling from export controls.

### AMD — Advanced Micro Devices
**Layer(s):** L4  
**Tier:** CRITICAL  
**Role:** Primary GPU alternative to Nvidia; also leading CPU supplier for agentic AI orchestration. $100B 6GW AMD-Meta deal is the largest hyperscaler chip commitment with a non-Nvidia GPU supplier. EPYC Venice (Zen 6, TSMC N2, 256 cores/512 threads) has the highest thread count of any server CPU — well-positioned for the agentic CPU surge. Two consecutive price increase rounds planned (Q2+Q3 2026, cumulative 16–17%).  
**Competitive Position:** GPU business ~1–2 generations behind NVDA in software ecosystem maturity but contracted at scale. CPU business is genuinely competitive with Intel given agentic demand surge.  
**Nearest Alternatives:** NVDA (GPU dominant incumbent). Intel (CPU). Custom ASICs (growing, ~40% of AI server shipments by 2030).  
**Key Risks:** CUDA ecosystem lock-in limits GPU penetration beyond committed deals. N3 allocation competition with NVDA and hyperscaler ASICs.

### AMKR — Amkor Technology
**Layer(s):** L3  
**Tier:** CRITICAL  
**Role:** Leading OSAT globally; primary beneficiary of CoWoS packaging overflow as TSMC's internal capacity is insufficient for AI accelerator demand. Geographic diversification demand (US/Korea fabs) adds a second growth vector. CHIPS Act tailwinds for US facility expansion.  
**Competitive Position:** Direct overflow beneficiary from TSMC's CoWoS constraint. Long-term customer relationships with major AI chip designers.  
**Nearest Alternatives:** ASX (ASE Technology) — other major OSAT.  
**Key Risks:** Revenue tied to TSMC CoWoS capacity decisions. Technology gap vs. TSMC in the most advanced packaging formats.

### ARM — Arm Holdings
**Layer(s):** L2, L4  
**Tier:** CRITICAL  
**Role:** Architecture royalty on every Arm-based chip shipped — including hyperscaler custom silicon (Graviton, Trainium, Cobalt, TPU) built to reduce NVDA dependency. ~40% of cloud data center market, 1B+ deployed Neoverse cores. Now producing own silicon: 136-core AGI CPU (TSMC 3nm, co-designed with Meta); air-cooled rack 8,160 cores/36kW; liquid-cooled rack 45,000+ cores/200kW. Arm/NVDA collaboration allows AI agents to autonomously assign workloads across both architectures. Verda (neocloud) first to deploy alongside GB300/VR200 racks.  
**Competitive Position:** Royalty on the custom silicon trend itself — every hyperscaler escaping NVDA uses Arm. AGI CPU adds a direct revenue vector while the licensing business benefits from the same trend.  
**Nearest Alternatives:** RISC-V (open-source, years from mainstream data center adoption). x86 (declining share in custom silicon).  
**Key Risks:** RISC-V adoption timeline is the primary long-term threat. Royalty rate negotiations with hyperscalers who have leverage.

### AVGO — Broadcom

**Tier:** CRITICAL  
**Role:** Primary custom ASIC supplier to Google (TPU v7/v8 Ironwood/Sunfish) and Meta; dominant networking switch silicon provider. Long-term agreement with Google through 2031 to develop and supply future generations of custom AI chips and rack components. Anthropic separately secured 3.5GW of Google TPUs starting 2027 through a Broadcom/Google deal.

**Nearest Alternatives:** Marvell (AWS Trainium, Azure). Alchip and MediaTek growing in design volume (underfollowed); Google is explicitly maintaining a dual-sourcing strategy with MediaTek through at least TPU v10.  
**Key Risks:** Customer concentration in a small number of hyperscalers. Custom ASIC design cycles are long — program cancellation creates multi-year revenue gap.

### BE — Bloom Energy
**Layer(s):** L8  
**Tier:** CRITICAL  
**Role:** Leading solid oxide fuel cell (SOFC) manufacturer for behind-the-meter AI data center power. Oracle commitment: up to 2.8GW for Project Jupiter (Doña Ana County NM, $165B campus, hosting OpenAI) — Oracle is using Bloom as its sole energy provider for the project. AEP signed separate 1GW SOFC deal. Equinix deploying across 19 data centers at 100MW+. $5B infrastructure partnership with Brookfield. SOFCs are fuel-agnostic (natural gas/biogas/hydrogen), 800V DC compatible, paired with closed-loop non-evaporative cooling. Q1 2026: adjusted EPS 44 cents (vs. 12-cent estimate), revenue $751M (+130% YoY, beating $539M estimate). Full-year 2026 guidance raised to $3.4B–$3.8B revenue, $1.85–$2.25/share earnings. Shares +231% in 2026; record close $287.97 (+27% on earnings). Morgan Stanley price target raised to $310.  
**Competitive Position:** Behind-the-meter generation is becoming mainstream — Oracle 2.8GW + AEP 1GW represent the clearest public proof. Fuel-agnostic and water-efficient design uniquely suited to constrained AI campus environments. CEO KR Sridhar framing: "speed-to-power" for hyperscalers is worth massive cost premiums over grid power.  
**Nearest Alternatives:** Gas turbines (GEV, Siemens — different use case, grid-scale vs. behind-the-meter). Solaris Energy Infrastructure (SEI) — mobile gas turbines for behind-the-meter. Plug Power (hydrogen fuel cells — weaker financially).  
**Key Risks:** SOFC costs higher than grid power; viability depends on grid unavailability. Historical profitability challenges — contract scale improving but execution risk persists. Valuation is parabolic — 14 "Buy" vs. 13 "Hold" analyst ratings reflect a near-even split on risk/reward at current levels.

### BESI — Besi (BE Semiconductor Industries)
**Layer(s):** L2  
**Tier:** CRITICAL  
**Role:** Hybrid bonding equipment manufacturer — the packaging technology required for HBM4 and next-generation advanced memory. SK Hynix ordered the joint Applied Materials/Besi hybrid bonding inline system (~$15M / KRW 20B) — first mass-production-intent hybrid bonding purchase. Samsung also adopted Besi for development. Hybrid bonding production adoption expected at HBM4 (H2 2026 into 2027). Listed on Euronext Amsterdam.  
**Competitive Position:** First-mover in hybrid bonding equipment for HBM mass production. SEMES (Samsung internal) tools noted as less mature. Besi is the external market standard.  
**Nearest Alternatives:** SEMES (less mature). Applied Materials (partner on joint system, not direct competitor for the bonder itself).  
**Key Risks:** Euronext listing — US liquidity may be constrained. Hybrid bonding ramp timeline depends on HBM4 demand; any Rubin or HBM yield delay pushes out the revenue event.

### CDNS — Cadence Design Systems
**Layer(s):** L2  
**Tier:** CRITICAL  
**Role:** EDA software duopolist alongside Synopsys — required for designing every advanced AI chip. Every AI accelerator (NVDA, AMD, hyperscaler custom silicon) requires Cadence tools. AI chip design boom is a direct revenue driver; more chips being designed = more Cadence licenses consumed. Switching costs extremely high — designers build years of flows around specific EDA toolchains.  
**Competitive Position:** Cadence + Synopsys together >60% of global EDA market and >70% of IP market.  
**Nearest Alternatives:** Synopsys (the other half of the duopoly — both are necessary). Mentor Graphics (Siemens EDA) is distant third.  
**Key Risks:** Export controls on China limit TAM. Synopsys-Ansys merger (if completed) strengthens the competitor.

### COHR — Coherent Corp

**Role:** Leading gas turbine and grid equipment manufacturer; oligopolist alongside Siemens Energy. Q1 2026: orders $18.3B (+71% YoY, nearly 2x quarterly revenue), EPS $17.44 vs. $1.97 consensus (9x beat), $200B backlog target by end 2026 (two years ahead of schedule), 100GW under contract. **Key Risks:** Stock up 200%+ in prior 12 months before Q1 print — significant appreciation already. Execution risk on $200B backlog. A major deceleration in data center builds would extend (not cancel) backlog duration.

### GLW — Corning
**Layer(s):** L7, L3*  
**Tier:** CRITICAL  
**Role:** Dominant fiber optic cable manufacturer — direct beneficiary of AI data center fiber demand surge (hyperscaler-backed rural backbone expansion, dark fiber buildout). Zayo's 8,000 route-mile (15M fiber-mile) network expansion directly backed by a major AI infrastructure customer validates GLW's demand runway. **Research priority (L3):** Corning's glass manufacturing expertise warrants investigation as a potential Layer 3 position — glass core substrates and glass interposers are the next-generation advanced packaging material as organic substrates hit physical limits. **Nearest Alternatives:** Prysmian, CommScope in fiber cable. Glass substrate angle is uninvestigated — key open question.  
**Key Risks:** Fiber cable market is more competitive than equipment layers. Glass substrate investment thesis is unconfirmed — research priority, not confirmed position.

### INTC — Intel
**Layer(s):** L3, L4  
**Tier:** CRITICAL  
**Role:** Dual role: (1) recovering foundry — yields improving 7%+/month, already hitting year-end targets ahead of schedule; Tesla (14A node, first named customer), Google (multiyear CPU + IPU co-development), Apple reportedly evaluating 18A-P for M-series (2027 — unconfirmed, described as "weighing/evaluating"); EMIB advanced packaging reached 90% yield milestone (vs. TSMC CoWoS ~98%); Google confirmed using EMIB for TPU v8e (2H 2027 production); Meta custom CPU confirmed using EMIB (2H 2028 production); Nvidia Feynman rumored (unconfirmed); CFO stated Intel is "close to closing" multiple billion-dollar EMIB packaging deals; EMIB advantages: >8x reticle size, scales to 12→24 HBM stacks, node-agnostic (bridges chips from different foundries); (2) CPU demand surge beneficiary — data center revenue +22% YoY, 27% ASP increase on 5% fewer units shipped (pure pricing power), ASIC segment doubled YoY to ~$1B, now monetizing edge-bin dies (previously scrapped). **Key Risks:** 18A yields below 50% — mass production remains 2027+ story. EMIB yield (90%) still trails TSMC CoWoS (~98%) — meaningful gap for highest-volume production. Apple foundry evaluation is exploratory, not confirmed. Net losses remain substantial.

### NVDA — Nvidia
**Layer(s):** L4, L6, L12  
**Tier:** CRITICAL  
**Role:** Dominant GPU and AI accelerator platform. CUDA ecosystem is the de facto standard — developer lock-in via years of CUDA-native code makes switching costly. GPU demand sold out through August–September 2026; H100 spot +40% from October 2025 trough ($2.35/hr). Vera Rubin NVL72 pricing gap: $4.92/hr cost floor vs. $12.25/hr value ceiling — substantial untapped pricing power not yet exercised. Software-only optimizations (wideEP, disaggregation, MTP) can 14x B300 throughput. Also: physical AI simulation (Omniverse/Isaac), nuclear digital twin partnerships, $6B interconnect investment sweep (Marvell, Coherent, Lumentum) extending architectural control into networking.  
**Competitive Position:** CUDA ecosystem moat is the primary defense. Google Virgo networking enables 960,000-GPU single-fabric deployment with NVDA Vera Rubin.  
**Nearest Alternatives:** AMD MI-series (credible, contracted with Meta $100B). Custom ASICs (TPU, Trainium — growing to ~40% of AI server shipments by 2030). Near-term, supply exhaustion makes alternatives largely theoretical for 2026.  
**Key Risks:** Custom silicon displacement is the primary long-term structural risk over 3–5 year horizon. Any major CUDA defection by a top-5 hyperscaler would be a high-signal event. Tier is CRITICAL not IRREPLACEABLE because AMD and custom ASICs are real, revenue-generating alternatives — the moat is durable but not absolute.
