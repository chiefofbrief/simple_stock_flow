**Q1. What is the mainstream narrative?**

The mainstream narrative frames TSMC as irreplaceable AI infrastructure — the sole manufacturer capable of producing advanced AI silicon at scale. News coverage from Feb–May 2026 (60 stories total: 30 Perigon, 30 FMP; average composite Perigon sentiment +0.287, 23 positive vs. 7 negative) organizes around three dominant themes:

**Theme 1 — Supply constraint as structural scarcity premium.** The most commercially significant narrative is physical production ceiling. A Broadcom executive publicly stated TSMC is "hitting (production capacity) limits" and that capacity through 2027 "has become a bottleneck, or that has kind of choked the supply chain in 2026" [CONFIRMED: Perigon, 2026-04-01, Reuters]. Customers are signing multi-year supply agreements of 3–4 years and paying 50–100% premiums for priority 3nm access [CONFIRMED: Perigon, 2026-03-31]. Cloud service providers are personally lobbying CEO C.C. Wei for supply [CONFIRMED: Perigon, 2026-03-31]. This creates a qualitative scarcity premium narrative layered on top of the earnings story.

**Theme 2 — AI demand acceleration.** Coverage consistently frames TSMC as the "pick-and-shovel play for the entire tech industry" and "best pick-and-shovel growth stock for the AI supercycle" [CONFIRMED: FMP, 2026-04-06 Motley Fool; 2026-03-07 Motley Fool]. 2nm capacity projected at 70% CAGR 2026–2028; CoWoS advanced packaging at 80%+ CAGR; Arizona output to grow 80% in 2026 vs. 2025 [CONFIRMED: Perigon, 2026-04-30, TSMC Technology Symposium]. Q1 2026 earnings (net income +58% YoY, revenue +35% YoY) drove shares to a record and pushed market cap past $2 trillion [CONFIRMED: Perigon, 2026-04-29]. The shift to agentic AI is cited as a new incremental demand step-up beyond prior HPC/LLM buildout narrative.

**Theme 3 — Geopolitical risk and diversification pressure.** Apple is in early exploratory talks with Intel and Samsung as TSMC backup suppliers, driven by tightening AI-driven supply availability and Taiwan concentration risk [CONFIRMED: Perigon, 2026-05-06, Bloomberg]. Apple CEO Tim Cook confirmed on the Q2 FY2026 earnings call that TSMC 3nm is Apple's primary production bottleneck: supply chain flexibility is "materially tighter than normal" and Mac mini/Mac Studio supply constrained "several months" [CONFIRMED: Perigon, 2026-05-06]. A trade-secrets theft conviction (10-year sentence for 2nm process theft involving Tokyo Electron Taiwan) reinforces IP and process concentration risk [CONFIRMED: Perigon, 2026-04-29]. US tariff risk and export controls on China sales are recurring concerns [CONFIRMED: multiple Perigon stories].

**Analyst Q&A questions** confirm the professional community is concentrated on: supply constraint duration and capacity ramp pace; N3 gross margin crossover timing and depreciation impact; CapEx trajectory vs. revenue growth; margin sustainability at 66%+; Terafab/customer vertical integration threats; Arizona fab economics vs. Taiwan; AI revenue forecasting methodology; advanced packaging (CoWoS/SoIC/CoPoS) strategy; and Samsung LPU competitive wins. The dominant analyst concern is valuation — whether the premium structure and AI demand thesis are already fully priced in.

Named customers surfacing in news: Apple (confirmed major N3 customer, Tim Cook on Q2 FY2026 call), Nvidia (largest customer, overtook Apple per news), AMD (smaller N3 allocation), Google (TPU v8), AWS (Trainium3), Meta (through AMD/custom silicon). **Part A — Metric Analysis**

**Revenue**

Quarterly momentum is strong and sequential: NTD 933.79B → 989.92B → 1,055.98B → 1,145.82B across the past four quarters (+11.3%, +6.0%, +6.7%, +8.5%), with each successive quarter setting a new record [CONFIRMED: `TSM_financial_analysis.md`, quarterly table].

The geographic concentration (North America 76% of Q1 2026 revenue [CONFIRMED: `TSM_mda_excerpts.md`]) and platform concentration (HPC 61%, Smartphone 26% [CONFIRMED: same]) reflect genuine customer demand, not circular revenue: NVDA, Apple, AMD, and hyperscaler custom silicon collectively drive the North American total, and all are demonstrated external arm's-length customers. However, news confirms multi-year supply agreements being signed with major customers at premium prices [CONFIRMED: Perigon, 2026-03-31], which is consistent with growing committed demand that will convert to revenue as capacity is delivered. Revenue growth tracks AI infrastructure investment directly: HPC grew +20% sequentially in Q1 2026 [CONFIRMED: `TSM_mda_excerpts.md`], consistent with the hyperscaler CapEx acceleration documented in the supply chain index and news. No red flags in revenue quality; no flag needed for Category 1 investigation. AMAT comparison: AMAT's 5-year revenue CAGR of 5.3% with CV 0.08 is far slower and far more stable — confirming TSMC is in a structurally different demand environment [CONFIRMED: `TSM_financial_analysis.md`, AMAT section].

**Operating Margin**

TSMC's operating margin has expanded dramatically: 40.9% (2021) → 49.5% (2022) → 42.6% (2023, cyclical trough) → 45.7% (2024) → 50.8% (2025) → 53.3% TTM → 58.0% Q1 2026 [CONFIRMED: `TSM_financial_analysis.md`]. The 5-year CV of 0.09 is exceptionally low — a business with this scale of earnings showing this level of margin stability demonstrates structural rather than cyclical profitability. The single-quarter Q1 2026 operating margin of 58.0% is the highest in the available record and represents a 7.5% sequential improvement, confirming that operating leverage is actively materializing: revenue grew 8.5% sequentially while operating income grew proportionally faster [CONFIRMED: same, quarterly table]. The MD&A confirms: "Operating margin improved 4.1 percentage points sequentially to 58.1% in 1Q26" with drivers cited as cost improvement efforts, higher utilization, and favorable FX [CONFIRMED: `TSM_mda_excerpts.md`].

The 5-year CAGR of operating margin is 5.5% per year, meaning margins are not just sustained but persistently expanding. This is consistent with the supply chain index characterization: TSMC's structural position allows cost improvement as new nodes mature and depreciation curves turn favorable. The 2023 trough (42.6%) was driven by underutilization — fixed costs spread over a smaller revenue base — and the subsequent recovery confirms the thesis that TSMC's margin structure is utilization-sensitive, not structurally impaired. At current utilization approaching 100% on N3 [CONFIRMED: supply chain index], margins are at structural highs with the N3 depreciation crossover still ahead. AMAT comparison: AMAT's operating margin is consistently ~29%, flat over 5 years (CV 0.02) — a mature, competitive equipment business with no structural pricing power [CONFIRMED: `TSM_financial_analysis.md`, AMAT section]. TSMC's current 53–58% operating margin is nearly double AMAT's, reflecting monopolistic pricing power at the foundry layer.

**Operating Cash Flow**

OCF has grown from NTD 1,112B (2021) to NTD 2,383B (2025), with TTM OCF of NTD 2,397B (~$77.3B USD), a 5-year CAGR of 21.0% [CONFIRMED: `TSM_financial_analysis.md`]. The CV of 0.31 is higher than revenue's 0.34 because OCF is more sensitive to working capital timing. The FY2023 trough (-22.9% YoY) tracked the revenue cycle, confirming that OCF is operationally driven. Quarterly OCF is visibly volatile: NTD 497.06B (Q2 2025, -20.5%), 426.83B (Q3 2025, -14.1%), 741.73B (Q4 2025, +73.8%), 731.50B (Q1 2026, -1.4%) [CONFIRMED: same]. This quarterly pattern reflects working capital timing across the semiconductor supply chain — OCF is lumpy quarter-to-quarter but the annual and TTM trend is unambiguously upward. The quarterly volatility does not impair the annual picture: FY2025 OCF of NTD 2,383B is the highest in the record and TTM of 2,397B is marginally higher still. AMAT comparison: AMAT's TTM OCF of $8.72B USD is roughly 11% of TSMC's on a USD basis, despite having been in operation for decades longer — the foundry layer generates dramatically more cash than the equipment layer [CONFIRMED: `TSM_financial_analysis.md`, AMAT section].

**Free Cash Flow**

FCF (OCF minus CapEx) shows the highest CAGR (43.0%) and highest volatility (CV 0.60) of any metric in the analysis [CONFIRMED: `TSM_financial_analysis.md`]. Annual FCF: NTD 262.72B (2021) → 520.97B (2022) → 286.57B (2023, trough -45.0%) → 870.17B (2024, +203.7%) → 1,097.58B (2025) → TTM 1,102.16B (~$35.6B USD) [CONFIRMED: same]. The high volatility reflects that FCF = OCF − CapEx, and CapEx is lumpy: when CapEx dipped in 2023–2024 (both ~NTD 955–956B) while OCF recovered, FCF jumped disproportionately. The TTM FCF of NTD 1,102B represents a 26.7% FCF margin on TTM revenue, which is substantial for a capital-intensive foundry — but requires critical context. CapEx at NTD 1,295B TTM (~$41.8B USD) includes substantial growth investment (2nm buildout, Arizona/Japan overseas fabs) that is not maintenance spending. [ESTIMATED: TTM CapEx NTD 1,295B / TTM D&A NTD 682B = 1.90x, implying growth investment ≈ 90% of D&A = ~NTD 613B of incremental growth CapEx beyond replacement]. Maintenance FCF (OCF minus maintenance CapEx, proxied as D&A) would be approximately NTD 2,397B − 682B = NTD 1,715B (~$55.3B USD) — materially higher than reported FCF and a more relevant "owner earnings" figure if the growth investment is discretionary. The quarterly FCF pattern mirrors OCF: NTD 197.50B, 139.38B, 388.17B, 377.11B in the past four quarters — positive in every quarter, no quarters with FCF burn [CONFIRMED: `TSM_financial_analysis.md`].

**OCF / Net Income**

This metric declined from 1.88x (2021) to 1.24x TTM, a 5-year CAGR of -7.5%, CV 0.12 [CONFIRMED: `TSM_financial_analysis.md`]. This was the primary focus question from Context — and the decomposition resolves it as a mechanical issue, not an earnings quality problem.

Bridge analysis [ESTIMATED from `TSM_financial_analysis.md`, method: NI derived from OCF / ratio]:
- TTM NI: NTD 2,397B / 1.24 = ~NTD 1,933B (~$62.4B USD; consistent with ~$12.04 ADR EPS × ~5.18B shares)
- The gap between OCF (2,397B) and NI (1,933B) = NTD 464B is comprised of:
  - D&A addback: ~NTD 682B (positive — increases OCF above NI)
  - Working capital consumption: approximately NTD -218B (negative — reduces OCF relative to NI)
  - The net is the NTD 464B excess of OCF over NI

The key question is why the ratio is declining from 1.88x to 1.24x. Three mechanical explanations:

1. **NI growing faster than OCF.** [ESTIMATED: NI 5yr CAGR ~31% vs OCF 5yr CAGR 21%] Net income is growing faster because (a) operating margins are expanding (NI benefits fully), (b) D&A/Revenue is declining (see CapEx section), meaning NI is growing faster than the cash equivalent.

2. **D&A addback declining as % of NI.** As margins expand, NI grows faster than D&A. In 2021, D&A/NI was approximately NTD 422B / 591B = 71%; in 2025, approximately NTD 695B / 1,739B = 40%. D&A is a smaller fraction of NI than in 2021, so the ratio mechanically compresses.

3. **Working capital consumption.** WC grew at 28.2% CAGR vs revenue at 24.8% [CONFIRMED: `TSM_financial_analysis.md`], meaning cash is being consumed in WC at a modestly faster rate than revenue growth. This WC build likely reflects receivables scaling with revenue and cash accumulation on the balance sheet, not a dangerous inventory build or collection problem.

The Q3 2025 dip to 0.94x (below 1.0x) is a one-quarter timing anomaly — OCF was unusually low in Q3 (NTD 426.83B) before recovering to 741.73B in Q4 2025, consistent with working capital timing around contract terms, not a structural issue [CONFIRMED: `TSM_financial_analysis.md`].

SBC contribution is negligible: SBC/Revenue is effectively 0% throughout the 5-year record (max 0.1% in 2024) [CONFIRMED: `TSM_financial_analysis.md`, SBC rows]. The SBC data shows some FMP data irregularities (FY2025 showing $0, TTM showing $0.62B) but even at the higher figure this is <0.02% of TTM revenue — immaterial [CONFIRMED: same]. This is a genuine quality differentiator vs. US tech companies where SBC distorts the OCF/NI ratio significantly.

**Conclusion on OCF/NI:** The declining ratio reflects legitimate structural mechanics — margin expansion causing NI to grow faster than OCF, D&A becoming a proportionally smaller addback, and moderate WC build. There is no evidence of earnings quality deterioration. GAAP earnings are confirmed by exceptional ROIC (see below). AMAT comparison: AMAT's OCF/NI of 1.11x TTM is lower than TSMC's 1.24x, reflecting higher SBC (2.4% of revenue vs TSMC's ~0%) [CONFIRMED: `TSM_financial_analysis.md`, AMAT section].

**Accounting Analysis**

**1. Do the footnotes/MD&A reveal anything material not captured in the financial statements?**

From available MD&A excerpts: three items merit note beyond the financial statements. First, the EUI/CIP depreciation commencement policy — management has discretion over when new fab equipment begins depreciating, and this is not visible in the income statement figures [CONFIRMED: `TSM_mda_excerpts.md`]. Second, the inventory policy around "normal capacity utilization" — when actual utilization exceeds normal (as now), inventory is carried at below-replacement cost, which is conservative but means cost of goods sold is understated relative to replacement cost in high-utilization periods [CONFIRMED: same]. Third, the deferred income tax asset discussion indicates TSMC uses tax holidays and surtax structures that affect the effective tax rate [CONFIRMED: `TSM_mda_excerpts.md`, deferred tax section] — the full tax position is not resolvable from available excerpts but is standard for a Taiwanese manufacturer with government semiconductor incentives. The JASM JV minority interests (from news) represent off-financial-statement equity contributions that reduce TSMC's net capital requirement for Japan expansion — a positive not captured in the CapEx figure alone.

**2. Do the footnotes/MD&A confirm or challenge the conclusions from the financial analysis?**

Available MD&A confirms: (1) revenue recognition is conservative and consistent with reported growth quality; (2) inventory policy is also conservative under high-utilization conditions; (3) the fixed-cost structure of fabs is explicitly acknowledged in the inventory policy language ("a significant amount of manufacturing costs is fixed") [CONFIRMED: `TSM_mda_excerpts.md`], validating the operating leverage analysis. The EUI/CIP policy creates a timing challenge to the D&A/Revenue declining trend — it does not challenge the conclusion that earnings are real, but it means near-term earnings may be moderately benefiting from deferred D&A recognition on newly installed equipment. This is an accounting timing risk, not an earnings quality risk.

**3. Do the footnotes/MD&A reveal any accounting choices that appear to be inflating or depressing reported earnings, cash flow, or balance sheet figures?**

One item: EUI/CIP depreciation timing. Management judgment about when new fab equipment is "ready for intended use" can defer D&A, modestly inflating reported earnings in periods of heavy CapEx. This is the most significant accounting choice in the available data. Quantification is not possible without the full fixed-asset schedule (not available in excerpts). However, the impact is bounded: even if 20% of NTD 1,295B TTM CapEx were in EUI/CIP status with a 12-month useful-life delay, the additional quarterly D&A would be ~NTD 1,295B × 20% × (1/10yr) = NTD 26B/quarter — approximately 2.3% of NTD 1,145B quarterly revenue and ~4.5 percentage points of operating margin. This is meaningful but would not change the fundamental investment conclusion. No other accounting choices appear to be inflating reported figures. The declining D&A/Revenue trend (16.5% TTM) is primarily operating leverage, with a secondary EUI/CIP timing contribution that is unquantifiable from available data.

**4. Are there any disclosures that appear incomplete, inconsistent with the financial statements, or warrant deeper investigation?**

Two gaps: (1) Customer concentration — no individual customer revenue percentages disclosed in formal filings (TSMC's 20-F does not require the same Regulation S-K concentration disclosures as US domestic issuers). This is a structural disclosure gap for foreign private issuers, not an inconsistency. The geographic and platform breakdowns partially substitute. (2) VIE and advance payment footnotes — unavailable due to absence of the full notes file. These are standard disclosures that would be in the 20-F footnotes but cannot be verified from the available excerpts. Neither gap constitutes an inconsistency with what is available — they are limitations of the data, not red flags in the data.
