#### Section 6: Preliminary Hypothesis

**Numbers**

Expect Pass 1 to confirm a capital-light, exceptionally high-ROIC business. Based on the MD&A: OCF of $3.0B in a single quarter, operating margins of 58–61%, EPS CAGR of 23% with no down years, and consistent beats. The key questions for the numbers: (1) What is ROIC on invested capital — and does it support the "moat" interpretation? (2) Is OCF/NI above 1.0x, confirming conservative accounting? (3) Are the rebates/incentives ($5,639M, +23%) creating a structural headwind to net revenue quality? (4) What does debt/FCF look like — can the $14B buyback be sustained? (5) Is SBC a meaningful drag on owner earnings vs. the adj EPS figure? The anticipated picture: a very high quality business with ROIC likely in the 30-60% range, strong FCF generation, and a balance sheet capable of sustaining aggressive capital return. The key risk to this picture is whether the rebates/incentives line is compressing the net economics of the payment network faster than VAS can offset.

**Narrative & Catalyst**

A dislocation narrative is clearly forming — multiple professional analysts have explicitly named the stock as "oversold" and identified the Q1 beat as the pivot point. The Berkshire exit created a sentiment dent, but Berkshire's departure from positions it held for years has occurred in other contexts without signaling business deterioration (e.g., portfolio concentration rationale). Analyst consensus remains at +37% implied upside. The near-term catalyst is Q2 2026 earnings (July 30) and the medium-term catalyst is the stablecoin settlement rollout and BVNK integration. The path to realization appears plausible over a 12-24 month timeframe if: (a) Q2 earnings continue the beat trajectory; (b) stablecoin fears prove manageable as MA embeds itself in the infrastructure; (c) no material legislative action on interchange economics.

**Scenario**

At 27.73x GAAP P/E, the current price appears to embed **a scenario between base case and mild bear case** — specifically, it assumes either meaningful moderation of the 23% EPS CAGR or structural margin compression from rebates and regulatory pressure, not necessarily business destruction. The price does NOT require an extraordinary outcome to be "fair" — it is pricing in enough risk to pull the P/E from its 5yr historical average of 38.6x down to 27.7x. What is NOT priced in: continued 20%+ EPS growth at a re-rated multiple returning toward historical norms. If EPS continues growing at even 15-18% (below the CAGR) and the P/E reverts even partially toward 33-35x, the return profile is substantial.

**Thesis**

**LOSER — EPS+.** Mastercard is a high-quality compounder experiencing multiple compression driven entirely by sentiment fears — stablecoin disruption, regulatory pressure, Berkshire exit — rather than fundamental deterioration. The business is accelerating: VAS +22%, EPS +23% YoY, Q1 beat on all metrics, $3B OCF in a single quarter. At 27.73x GAAP P/E vs a 5yr historical average of 38.6x, the current price appears to price in significant permanent structural damage that has not materialized and may not. The company is not passively absorbing the stablecoin threat — it is actively positioning into the infrastructure (BVNK, stablecoin settlement, Agent Pay). **Evidence that would confirm the thesis:** Pass 1 ROIC above 30%, OCF/NI above 1.1x, rebates/incentives growth tracking controllably, no material earnings inflection downward. **Evidence that would break the thesis:** ROIC significantly below historical levels, rebates growing materially faster than revenue for 2+ consecutive years, clear legislative action on interchange economics (CCCA passage), or EPS growth inflecting below 10% with no recovery catalyst.

**Q20. Pass 1 focus questions**

1. What is ROIC, and has it been stable/improving? A business commanding this multiple historically must demonstrate sustained high returns on invested capital — any deterioration is the primary valuation risk.
2. Is OCF/NI above 1.0x? The adj EPS narrative depends on cash earnings being real. Verify.
3. Rebates & incentives: Are they growing faster than payment network net revenue on a sustained basis, and if so, what does this imply for future net revenue growth rates?
4. What is the true FCF after SBC? The GAAP vs adj gap is minimal (3.4%), but SBC as an absolute dollar figure matters for owner earnings calculation at this market cap.
5. What does the implied growth rate in the current P/E require? At 27.7x, what EPS growth does the market need to justify this as a floor vs a trap?
