#### Section 6: Preliminary Hypothesis

**Numbers:**

Pass 1 is expected to confirm:
- Very high ROIC — Visa's capital-light, fee-based network model (no credit risk, pure processing) should generate exceptional ROIC well above 20%. The key question is whether ROIC is stable or being impacted by litigation-related cash outflows and recent acquisitions (Featurespace, Pismo, Prisma/Newpay totaling ~$3B+).
- High FCF conversion — the network model requires minimal CapEx; near-total earnings should translate to free cash flow. FCF/NI ratio should be above 1.0x unless litigation payments are distorting operating cash flows.
- The litigation provision is the primary GAAP/non-GAAP divergence driver. At ~8% gap currently (vs. ~12% in FY2025 when the full $2.56B provision hit), this is manageable. Pass 1 should confirm the litigation escrow account balance and assess whether it represents sunk cash or recoverable value.
- Consistent and accelerating EPS — the non-GAAP EPS trajectory (+14% FY2025, estimated higher for FY2026 TTM) should be clean. The GAAP EPS is distorted by litigation accruals that may or may not represent actual cash losses.

**Narrative & Catalyst:**

A clear narrative is forming around V — the "litigation discount" thesis is gaining traction in professional and retail circles, and the record Q2 2026 earnings provided a confirming data point. The catalyst path is: (1) continued strong earnings through FY2026 (next print July 28), (2) litigation progress — either settlement on the interchange MDL or favorable developments in the DOJ case — enabling multiple re-rating, (3) VAS growth accelerating toward 20-25%+ on a larger base. Berkshire's exit is the primary counter-signal and the one the market hasn't fully processed. The institutional narrative (Norges Bank buying, Mawer holding large) is firmly bullish and has more recent confirmation data.

Timeframe: 12–24 months for meaningful price realization if litigation disc ount normalizes.

**Scenario:**

The current price (~$320, ~25.7x adjusted TTM P/E) appears to embed a *base case with a persistent litigation discount*. The market is pricing Visa as though: (1) earnings will continue growing at 12-17%, and (2) the DOJ case will impose ongoing structural restrictions that permanently reduce the multiple from historical 43x toward a ~26x ceiling. This is neither the optimistic scenario (full business intact, multiple re-rates toward 30x+) nor the catastrophic scenario (DOJ wins broad injunctive relief forcing open debit routing). At ~26x with 20% EPS CAGR history, the stock offers a reasonable base case with substantial upside optionality if litigation risk proves excessive.

**Thesis:**

V is a LOSER—EPS+ candidate exhibiting a textbook price/earnings divergence driven by litigation fear. The business is delivering record performance — 20% EPS CAGR, 17% revenue growth, VAS accelerating at 29%+ — while the stock has declined ~14% from its 52-week high. The litigation overhang (DOJ debit monopolization + interchange MDL) is compressing the multiple from a historical average of ~43x to ~26x current. This compression, if excessive, represents a significant embedded return: re-rating from 26x back to even 32x (the 1-year historical average) — with no further earnings growth — would produce ~23% upside. With earnings growing at 12-17% annually, the 12-18 month total return potential is substantial.

**What would confirm the thesis:** (1) Pass 1 confirms exceptional ROIC and FCF conversion with no structural deterioration in network economics; (2) DOJ case shows limited ability to impose structural remedies (injunctive relief aimed at specific exclusionary contracts rather than network restructuring); (3) VAS growth sustainable above 20% post-World Cup and Olympics tailwinds.

**What would break the thesis:** (1) DOJ wins broad injunctive relief requiring open debit routing (CCCA-type framework) structurally reducing pricing power on debit; (2) large issuer clients defect on contract renewal, accelerating client incentive costs; (3) stablecoin/agent transaction volume begins materially cannibalizing core network volume at scale.

**Q20 — Pass 1 focus questions:**

1. What is V's ROIC (5-year trend)? Is it showing any deterioration that would suggest moat erosion, or holding above 20%+?
2. What is the FCF/NI ratio and FCF vs_1Y/vs_2Y trend? If FCF is declining despite rising EPS, this is an earnings quality flag.
3. How large is the litigation escrow account, and how much actual cash has been paid/deposited? Does the escrow represent real ongoing cash drain or is it primarily an accrual awaiting resolution?
4. What is the GAAP vs. non-GAAP EPS spread, and what drives it beyond litigation? SBC and acquisition amortization (Pismo, Featurespace, Prisma/Newpay) should be assessed separately from litigation.
5. Is there any evidence of network economics deterioration — revenue per transaction declining, client incentive ratio increasing faster than volume, or cross-border mix shift reducing yield?
6. What does P/OE (owner earnings, after SBC) show? In a capital-light network business, SBC can be a meaningful drag on true owner earnings even if reported EPS looks clean.

#### V Financial Analysis

**Metrics**

**Revenue**

Visa's revenue has compounded at 13.5% annually over the past 5 years (CV 0.19 — low volatility, highly consistent), growing from $24.11B in FY2021 to $43.03B TTM [CONFIRMED: V_financial_analysis.md]. The pattern is stable double-digit growth with no outlier year: +21.6% in FY2022 (post-COVID travel recovery), then steady 10-11% growth in FY2023-2025. Revenue quality is exceptionally high — in a payments network there is no channel stuffing, no receivables manipulation, and no inventory to misrepresent. Revenue is earned on transaction volume and client fees; the underlying drivers (payments volume, cross-border volume, processed transactions) are disclosed quarterly and cross-reference against independently observable economic activity. Quarterly revenue has been consistently advancing: $10.17B → $10.72B → $10.90B → $11.23B (Q3FY2025 through Q2FY2026), each quarter above the prior, with YoY growth of +17% in Q2FY2026 per the MD&A [CONFIRMED: V_mda_excerpts.md]. VAS revenue is the fastest-growing component: +43% "Other" revenues in FY2025, +29-31% VAS specifically in Q2FY2026 — growing at more than twice the core network rate [CONFIRMED: V_mda_excerpts.md]. Peer comparison: MA revenue CAGR 14.8% (5yr) vs. V 13.5% — MA is growing slightly faster off a smaller base; both are materially above typical payment sector peers. No revenue quality flags.

*TL;DR: Revenue is growing at a consistent 13-17% rate with no quality concerns, driven by broad-based volume growth and accelerating VAS. The core revenue engine is intact and strengthening.*

**Operating Margin**

GAAP operating margin 5yr average is 63.9% (CV 0.04 — remarkably stable), one of the highest sustained operating margins in global business [CONFIRMED: V_financial_analysis.md]. The FY2025 dip to 60.0% (from 65.7% in FY2024, -8.7%) is entirely litigation-driven: the $2,562M litigation provision ($2,100M higher than FY2024) accounts for approximately 5.25 percentage points of margin erosion [ESTIMATED: V_mda_excerpts.md 10-K FY2025 / $40.0B FY2025 revenue]. Quarterly trend: 60.7% → 57.3% → 61.8% → 64.4% — the Q4FY2025 (Sep) dip to 57.3% reflected an elevated litigation accrual quarter; the FY2026 quarters are trending toward structural highs. Peer comparison: MA TTM operating margin 59.4% vs. V 61.1% — V operates at a materially higher margin than its only true peer, reflecting V's larger network scale advantage and slightly better pricing power. The underlying business is generating ~64% operating margins on a normalized basis — a figure that has held essentially unchanged for 5 years despite material revenue growth, confirming the capital-light, network economics model.

*TL;DR: GAAP margins are temporarily depressed by litigation provision; normalized margins are 64-66% — unchanged and exceptional. No structural margin compression is occurring.*

**Free Cash Flow**

TTM FCF is $21.18B (CapEx $1.57B, OCF $22.76B) [CONFIRMED: V_financial_analysis.md]. 5yr CAGR: 10.4% (CV 0.14 — highly consistent). FCF is growing from $14.52B (FY2021) to $21.18B TTM — a $6.7B increase with minimal capital intensity. CapEx as a percentage of revenue is approximately 3.7% TTM ($1.57B / $43.03B) — extraordinary low for a technology infrastructure business at this scale. CapEx/D&A has been consistently above 1.0x (ranging 112-122% over 5 years), meaning Visa is investing above maintenance level — appropriate for a growing platform [CONFIRMED: V_financial_analysis.md].
