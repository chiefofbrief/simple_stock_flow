#### Section 1: Sentiment Landscape

**Q1. Mainstream narrative**

The dominant market narrative around Gartner (IT) is a **securities fraud class action + CV growth deceleration + AI disruption threat** triple-overhang, compounded by a "show me" posture after two successive earnings-related crashes.

The lawsuit is impossible to miss in the news data. From March through May 2026, a cascade of plaintiff law firms (Bernstein Liebhard, Rosen, Schall, Faruqi, Gross, Glancy, Kahn Swick, and others) issued near-identical press releases urging investors with losses to seek lead-plaintiff status before the May 18, 2026 deadline. [CONFIRMED: IT_news.md, multiple FMP articles, March 26 – April 26, 2026] The class period is February 4, 2025 through February 2, 2026. The complaint alleges that Gartner and two senior executives made materially false statements about contract value (CV) growth and consulting revenue outlook, and that two corrective disclosures — a -27.55% single-day drop on August 5, 2025, and a -20.87% drop on February 3, 2026 — constitute the damages events. [CONFIRMED: IT_news.md, "Gartner faces federal investor lawsuit over alleged misstatements," April 10, 2026 (TipRanks); "Gartner, Inc. Securities Fraud Class Action," March 26, 2026 (Perigon)]

The core business concern is **contract value growth**. CV is Gartner's primary leading indicator — subscriptions are recognized ratably, so today's CV is next year's revenue. CV grew only +1% FCN at both December 31, 2025 and March 31, 2026. [CONFIRMED: IT_mda_excerpts.md, 10-K and 10-Q] Analysts are focused on when and whether CV can re-accelerate to the "high single-digit" range management has repeatedly promised. Seventeen of the twenty-three Q1 2026 analyst questions directly or indirectly probe CV trajectory, federal headwind lapping, and long-term target credibility. [CONFIRMED: IT_qa_questions.md, Q1 2026 transcript]

AI disruption appears in the news flow as **background anxiety** rather than an acute catalyst. SentinelOne/Trend Micro articles cite Gartner projections as authoritative. A Perigon story from May 21, 2026 — "Gartner shares fall 5.6% amid valuation" — notes that despite Q1 2026 EPS of $3.32 beating the $2.99 consensus by 11%, the stock still fell 5.6% on May 13, 2026, with the "central question" framed as whether Gartner can defend its subscription model as generative AI matures. [CONFIRMED: IT_news.md, "Gartner shares fall 5.6% amid valuation," May 21, 2026 (Perigon)]

The gap between demonstrated results and narrative: **EPS is actually growing** (+17.3% Q1 YoY, GAAP diluted $3.18 vs $2.71) and OCF improved +25% YoY. The market is discounting the earnings quality because the leading indicator (CV) tells a different story — CV is barely growing (+1%), which means revenue growth will be muted or declining in 2026 regardless of what EPS shows. The market is rationally concerned about the fundamental trajectory even though current earnings look fine.

**Q8. Is the price drop an anomaly vs. the long-term trend?**

The price drop is NOT an anomaly — it represents a regime change, not a continuation of prior volatility. For 4+ years, Gartner was a steady compounder with a 5yr price CAGR of approximately +12-15% (before the recent crash). The current -4.1% 5yr CAGR is entirely a product of the 2025-2026 crash. **Q9. Is the price decline tracking real deterioration or overreaction?**

However, the *magnitude* of the price decline (-62%) vs. the TTM EPS decline (just -4.3%, from $14.15 to $13.55) is vastly disproportionate, suggesting:

(a) The market is pricing future CV-driven revenue deterioration (forward-looking), not just current earnings, and
(b) A risk discount for the litigation overhang, and
(c) An AI disruption discount.

The positive correlation means price is not purely an overreaction to good fundamentals. The market is pricing a genuine fundamental risk (CV), but may be overcorrecting.

Management explicitly flags four primary headwinds in the filings:

2. **AI disruption risk** (10-K Risk Factors): LLMs providing substantive content in search results reducing need for Gartner subscriptions; clients loading proprietary content into LLMs (contractually prohibited but unenforceable at scale); AI competitors potentially commoditizing research delivery. [CONFIRMED: IT_mda_excerpts.md, 10-K Risk Factors]

3. **Wallet retention deterioration**: Existing clients reducing spend ("lower levels of spending by existing clients"). GTS wallet retention -6pp over two years (from 102% to 96%); GBS -7pp (from 106% to 99%). [CONFIRMED: IT_mda_excerpts.md]

4. **Macroeconomic headwinds**: World Bank projects 2.6% global GDP growth in 2026, tilted to the downside; geopolitical uncertainty reducing enterprise decision velocity. [CONFIRMED: IT_mda_excerpts.md, 10-K]

**Gap vs. analyst/news focus:** News and analysts are highly focused on the litigation and federal headwind. Management's most significant but least-headline-grabbing risk is the **wallet retention decline** — existing clients reducing spend is a structural, multi-year problem that cannot be resolved by simply lapping the federal headwind. The market may be underweighting this relative to the discrete federal exposure. [INFERRED: IT_mda_excerpts.md, comparative analysis]

[CONFIRMED: IT_mda_excerpts.md, 10-K Risk Factors, AskGartner section]

The product appears positioned as a **retention tool** (making existing subscriptions stickier by improving UX), not a monetization initiative. [CONFIRMED: IT_qa_questions.md, Q2 2026Q1, Jeff Mueller]

**AI investment mode**: The 10-K characterizes AI development as requiring "additional investment and increased costs, including costs related to developing talent." No monetization timeline disclosed. No AI revenue contribution disclosed. [CONFIRMED: IT_mda_excerpts.md, 10-K]

**AI as threat:** Gartner explicitly acknowledges LLMs as a threat to its content distribution model — if enterprises can obtain substantive research answers from Claude, GPT, or Gemini without paying Gartner's subscription fees (~$5K–$30K+ per seat), that is an existential revenue question. However, the counter-argument is Gartner's advisory network, proprietary Magic Quadrant methodology, and peer benchmarking data — qualitative judgment products that LLMs cannot yet replicate with the same authority or auditability. [INFERRED: IT_mda_excerpts.md, 10-K Risk Factors, with context from news data on Gartner's research cited by enterprises and vendors alike]

Gartner is simultaneously a producer of AI research (cited extensively in industry coverage) and a business threatened by AI disruption of its delivery model. The research business is actually benefiting from AI adoption (enterprises need guidance on AI investment decisions more than ever), while the delivery mechanism faces competition from AI tools.

CIOs, CTOs, and CDOs need trusted guidance on AI vendors, AI strategy, and AI governance more than they've needed any guidance in 20 years. Gartner is uniquely positioned to monetize this — it is cited in virtually every major AI product launch as a validation signal (Arista, Hughes, SentinelOne, Trend Micro, CXApp all cite Gartner Magic Quadrant positions in news coverage during the analysis window). [INFERRED: price drop despite EPS beat; IT_news.md, May 21 2026 article framing "central question" as AI subscription defense]

However, the AI opportunity (demand for advisory services on AI decisions) is real and underrepresented in the news narrative. **The market may be overweighting the delivery-model threat and underweighting the demand-side opportunity.** This asymmetry is potentially the thesis — but only if CV can re-accelerate, demonstrating that enterprise AI demand is pulling clients into Gartner subscriptions rather than away.

#### Section 6: Preliminary Hypothesis

**Q21. Preliminary hypothesis**

**Numbers — what Pass 1 is expected to show:**

Based on the P/E framing (GAAP ~15.4x, non-GAAP ~11.65x), the pricing metrics (47.8% ROIC, 5/5 profitable, OCF growing), and the MD&A data, Pass 1 is expected to reveal:
- A genuinely high-quality business in terms of capital efficiency and cash generation
- Earnings quality that is mixed: GAAP EPS is depressed by the goodwill impairment (~$2/share) and the GAAP/adj gap of ~25-34% requires investigation into SBC and amortization
- FCF trajectory improving (+28.7% YoY per tracker) but the Digital Markets divestiture is a major one-time item confounding the trend
- The 47.8% ROIC is a strong quality indicator but must be verified — it implies a powerful competitive moat (pricing power in the subscription base)
- Debt: Debt/OCF of 2.4x and $800M in new bonds (2031 and 2035 Notes issued November 2025) — manageable but worth quantifying
- The central risk in the numbers is whether CV stagnation will translate into visible revenue and earnings pressure in H2 2026 and 2027

**Narrative & Catalyst:**

The narrative picture entering Pass 1 is weak but not absent. Professional community is parked at neutral. Retail is disengaged. No upgrade cycle has started. The near-term gates (Q1 beat, May 18 litigation deadline) have passed without producing a price recovery. The next catalyst is August 4, 2026 (Q2 earnings) — whether CV growth shows the promised H2 acceleration will be the thesis-confirming or thesis-breaking event.

The path to price realization requires: (1) CV growth demonstrably re-accelerating to mid-single digits by Q3 2026; (2) analyst upgrades from neutral to buy; (3) litigation either settling or de-escalating (no new negative developments). At that point, a re-rating from 15x toward 20-25x trailing earnings could produce significant upside (+30-60% from current). Timeframe: 12-18 months.

**Scenario — what the current price embeds:**

The price of ~$158 on GAAP TTM P/E of 15.4x and non-GAAP P/E of 11.65x embeds a **base-to-bear scenario** where:
- CV growth remains stagnant or continues to deteriorate
- AI disruption gradually erodes subscription pricing power
- Litigation results in material financial liability
- The 12% EPS CAGR medium-term target is not achievable

This is effectively a **"show me" price** — the market requires confirmation that CV is recovering before it will re-rate. The price is not pricing in extraordinary outcomes; it is pricing in continued disappointment from the leading indicators while giving minimal credit for the ROIC quality and current earnings delivery.

**Thesis:**

*IT is a high-quality research subscription business (47.8% ROIC, 5/5 profitable, ~77% Insights gross margins) experiencing a temporary but real leading-indicator slowdown (CV +1% FCN) compounded by federal exposure, litigation overhang, and AI disruption anxiety.
