Cavco Industries insider disclosures on Form 4 report that common shares were surrendered to cover tax withholding obligations when restricted stock units (RSUs) were released. One filing specifically notes the surrender of 63 shares of Cavco common stock for this purpose, described as a tax-withholding disposition tied to RSU vesting. These transactions occur because employees (or insiders) deliver shares to satisfy tax liabilities rather than paying taxes in cash, which can reduce the immediate value received from compensation and affect the timing of taxable income. The filings are required under U.S. securities law to report changes in beneficial ownership, providing transparency into insider trading activity. Investors typically review such disclosures to understand whether share activity suggests insider confidence or reflects routine tax-related withholding rather than a decision to sell in the open market.

**Key Points:**

- Another Form 4 filing uses standardized terminology for how withholding is paid: it describes the “payment of exercise price or tax liability by delivering securities,” reinforcing that the tax liability was settled through share delivery instead of cash payment.
- The Form 4 template and instructions included in the filings explain that filers must check whether a transaction was made under a “contract, instruction or written plan” intended to satisfy Rule 10b5-1(c)’s affirmative defense conditions—an additional detail investors may use to distinguish planned trades from other activity.
