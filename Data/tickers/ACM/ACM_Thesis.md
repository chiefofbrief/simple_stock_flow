#### Section 6: Preliminary Hypothesis

**Q19. Preliminary hypothesis**

**Numbers**
The financials are expected to show a genuinely profitable, growing professional services business with a structural earnings quality problem that is event-specific rather than systemic. Key expectations entering Pass 1:
- ROIC in the 15–25% range given asset-light professional services model (modest fixed assets vs. substantial backlog-driven revenue)
- OCF/NI ratio chronically below 1.0x due to claims/working capital management, but historically positive; the H1 FY2026 collapse likely represents a multi-year low
- Meaningful goodwill ($3.7B) vs. tangible book — likely negative tangible equity, which is common for acquisitive professional services firms but warrants confirmation
- Debt/OCF: normalized Debt/OCF at prior-year OCF levels (~$600-700M annual OCF) would suggest manageable leverage; at H1 annualized OCF (~$148M), it would appear elevated
- The 33% GAAP-to-adjusted EPS gap is the central earnings quality question — driven primarily by restructuring charges and SBC; the trend in this gap matters

**Narrative & Catalyst**
The narrative picture is constructive but nascent. A recovery story is beginning to form (Seeking Alpha, Zacks, GuruFocus), concentrated in financial media rather than retail. The primary catalyst is the August 3 Q3 earnings release. If H2 FCF is visibly recovering, the law firm investigations lose traction, analyst targets become realistic anchors again, and the stock re-rates. The narrative is moving in the right direction but has not reached inflection — that requires H2 FCF delivery. The 60-day timeline is proximate enough to anchor the thesis.

**Scenario**
The current price ($73.37) appears to embed a pessimistic scenario: that the FCF collapse is partially structural (i.e., the two legacy project claims represent real value impairment, not just timing), that the $400M FCF target will be missed, and that the 13x adjusted P/E is a fair multiple for a business with degraded cash conversion. The base case — FCF recovers to $350-400M, EPS lands at $5.90-6.10 — would suggest a multiple re-rating to 18-22x adj EPS ($106-134 range). The price does not require an extraordinary outcome to produce significant upside; it requires a normal outcome. The bear case (claims partially impaired, FCF guidance missed again, securities fraud suits create settlement costs) could sustain current or lower price levels.

**Thesis**
AECOM is a high-quality global infrastructure consultancy (record $26.2B backlog, 18% adj EPS CAGR, improving Americas margins at record 20.0% NSR) whose stock has been dislocated by a specific and named cash flow event: two legacy construction projects from FY2019-2020 whose claims resolution has dragged far beyond expectations, collapsing H1 FY2026 OCF by 98% YoY. The earnings line is intact and growing. The P/E has compressed from 22x to 13x (adj) / 17.5x (GAAP) — well below historical norms and absolute value thresholds. The investment thesis rests on: (1) the cash flow impairment being genuinely temporary and project-specific; (2) H2 FY2026 FCF recovery materializing as guided ($400M full year); (3) multiple re-rating as the overhang clears and the securities fraud narrative dissipates. Confirmation: Q3 earnings (Aug 3) showing FCF recovery, claims balance plateauing or declining. Break: further claims escalation beyond $680M, another FCF miss, OCF/NI structural deterioration in the 5-year view, or evidence the GAAP/adj gap has been masking real earnings quality issues.

**Accounting**

**1. Revenue Recognition**

AECOM recognizes revenue using the input method (costs incurred / total estimated costs) for fixed-price, GMP, and time-and-materials contracts. Percentage-of-completion is the standard approach but highly judgment-intensive [CONFIRMED: ACM_notes.md, line 1297]. Critical issue: claims revenue is included in the transaction price to the extent "a significant reversal would not be probable" [CONFIRMED: ACM_notes.md]. Under this standard, AECOM has recognized $680M in claims receivables as of Q2 FY2026 [CONFIRMED: line 5378] — revenue recognized before client approval or cash payment.

The FY2025 external auditors (PwC, per the 10-K) identified claims revenue recognition as a Key Audit Matter, stating: "Revenue recognition relating to claims is highly judgmental as the amount has not been approved by the customer and it requires the Company to prepare estimates of amounts expected to be recovered." Auditors performed a lookback analysis of claims settled in the current year vs. prior-year estimates [CONFIRMED: ACM_notes.md, lines 36-40]. The lookback methodology is a lagging control — it validates prior estimates only after settlement, by which time the revenue has already flowed through multiple periods' income statements.

The escalation from $180M to $680M over three years ($500M) is the primary earnings quality concern. If even 20% of the $680M balance were impaired ($136M pre-tax), the after-tax impact at ~25% would be ~$102M, or approximately $0.79/share — a material hit to reported EPS. The securities fraud investigations (three law firms) center precisely on whether management's disclosures adequately warned shareholders about this escalating risk.

DSO: 83 days at Mar 31, 2026 vs 74 days at Sep 30, 2025 [CONFIRMED: ACM_mda_excerpts.md] — expanding. Receivables factoring adds a pull-forward distortion: $319.4M is being sold to financial institutions, which understates true DSO. Unadjusted DSO (without factoring benefit) would be materially higher.

[INFERRED: ACM_mda.md, logic: FY2026 guidance $36M vs H1 actuals $41.5M; similar pattern from prior years]

### Synthesis

**Numbers**

AECOM is a genuinely improving professional services business — record $26.2B backlog, Americas operating margin at record 20.0% NSR, ROIC of 13.8% (above cost of capital and improving over 5 years), and asset-light CapEx (0.88% of revenue). However, the financial picture carries pre-existing complications that predate the Q2 FY2026 OCF collapse: OCF/NI has declined from 4.07x to 1.10x over five years as recognized-but-uncollected claims grew from $180M (FY2024) to $680M (Q2 FY2026); recurring restructuring charges ($40-100M annually) are systematically excluded from adjusted EPS, creating a persistent ~33% GAAP/adj gap; and at FY2026 FCF guidance ($400M) minus SBC, owner earnings P/OE is approximately 27x — not cheap. Normalized to FY2025 FCF ($680M), P/OE is approximately 15x — fair. The numbers support a good business at a fair price under normalized conditions, not a great business at a distressed price.

**Narrative & Catalyst**

A recovery narrative is forming in financial media (Seeking Alpha April 2026, Zacks June 2026, GuruFocus fair value framing) but remains exclusively institutional — no retail engagement, zero Reddit discourse. The catalyst is real, proximate (60 days), and has concrete post-period data supporting it — but the bar is high: market needs to see H2 OCF rate implying $400M is achievable, with claims balance not growing further.

**Scenario**

The current price ($73.37, GAAP P/E ~17.5x on ~$4.20 TTM GAAP EPS) appears to embed a partial-resolution scenario: the market is pricing that the claims will be eventually resolved but with meaningful delay, that H2 FCF will partially recover but miss the $400M target, and that the multiple will remain suppressed pending proof of cash conversion.
