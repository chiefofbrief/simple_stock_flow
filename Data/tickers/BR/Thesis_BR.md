#### A. Financials Analysis

**Revenue — Solid, consistent, no deterioration.**
Revenue has compounded at 8.4% over 5 years (CV: 0.12 — remarkably stable) reaching $7.32B TTM. Recent acceleration: Q3 FY2026 +8% YoY, nine months FY2026 +9% YoY. ICS grew +9% in the most recent quarter; GTO grew +5%. The revenue growth story is intact and not slowing at the top line.

Revenue mix by type (Q3 FY2026): Recurring $1,288M (66%), Distribution $593M (30%), Event-driven $73M (4%). Mix has been structurally stable — recurring revenues are growing at 7-8% consistently, event-driven is up +38% in Q3 (favorable), and distribution growth is largely postage pass-through inflation ($34M of the $38M distribution increase is postage rate pass-through).

**Operating Margin — Expanding at group level; diverging by segment.**
Group operating margin: 13.6% (2021) → 17.3% (FY2025) → 17.1% TTM. 5yr CAGR of +6.1%. At the segment level, the picture diverges and this is where the bear case has partial validity:

- **ICS** (core governance/communications business): Pre-tax margin declined from 16.0% to 15.0% over the nine months ending March 2026 (-100bps). Revenue grew 9% but earnings before income taxes grew only 2% ($9.2M on a $563M base). The culprit is cost of revenues growing faster than operating income — distribution/postage costs and acquisition integration expenses are compressing margins. This is real compression, but the driver (postage pass-through and acquisition integration) is distinct from a structural collapse in high-margin recurring revenue.

- **GTO** (capital markets / wealth tech): Pre-tax margin expanded from 12.8% to 16.1% over the same nine months (+330bps). EBT up +37% on +9% revenue growth — positive operating leverage. GTO is the growth engine accelerating.

**Operating Cash Flow and Free Cash Flow — Excellent quality.**
OCF 5yr CAGR: 16.3% (faster than revenue, confirming margin expansion). FCF 5yr CAGR: 18.3%. TTM FCF: $1.30B. OCF/NI: 1.24x TTM — earnings-to-cash conversion is healthy, meaning reported earnings are not being sustained by accruals. At the current price (~$154/share, ~116M shares = ~$17.9B market cap), FCF yield is approximately 7.3%. For a business growing FCF at 18%+ annually, that is a compelling valuation floor.

Note: OCF/NI is highly seasonal due to Broadridge's fiscal year structure. Q2 (Oct-Dec) shows an extremely low 0.26x while Q1 (July-Sept) shows 1.87x. This is structural, not a quality concern.

**The EPS Inflation Question — Bear Case CONFIRMED, but full picture is nuanced.**
This is the most important finding in Pass 1. The non-GAAP reconciliation (10-Q) reveals:

| Period | GAAP EPS | Adj EPS | Gap | Driver |
|--------|----------|---------|-----|--------|
| Q3 FY2026 (3 months to Mar 2026) | $2.36 | $2.72 | Adj > GAAP | Normal amortization add-back |
| 9 months FY2026 (Jul 2025–Mar 2026) | $6.18 | $5.81 | GAAP > Adj | $2.03/share digital asset GAIN |
| Prior year 9 months | $3.93 | $5.00 | Adj > GAAP | $0 digital asset gain |

The bear case is confirmed: **a $2.03 per share digital asset gain was recorded in the first half of FY2026 and is not present in the prior year, inflating GAAP EPS comparisons.** The nine-month GAAP growth of +57% ($6.18 vs $3.93) is almost entirely a mirage; adjusted growth is +16% ($5.81 vs $5.00).

However, the +16% adjusted growth is real and healthy. The bear case framing of "EPS inflated by one-time gains" is correct as a description, but doesn't undermine the underlying earnings power — it just means the headline GAAP numbers overstate momentum.

On a TTM basis, the P/E inversion (GAAP 16.3x < Adj 17.1x) directly reflects this: GAAP EPS (~$9.45) > Adj EPS (~$9.01) because the digital asset gain remains in the trailing window.

**The Revenue Mix Deterioration Claim — Partially refuted.**
The specific claim that "higher-margin products are falling drastically" is not supported by the data. Recurring revenues in every ICS product line are growing 5-10%:
- ICS Regulatory: +9-10% YoY
- ICS Data-Driven Fund Solutions: +4-9% YoY
- ICS Issuer: +7-8% YoY
- ICS Customer Comms: +5-6% YoY

The margin pressure in ICS is real but caused by **cost inflation** (postage pass-through, acquisitions, volume expenses), not by high-margin products declining. This is a lower-quality growth situation, but not the structural collapse the bear case implies.

**Closed Sales — The genuine red flag.**
Closed sales measure expected annual recurring revenue from newly signed contracts. They are declining:
- Q3 FY2026: $57.5M, -19% YoY
- Nine months FY2026: $146.8M, -16% YoY

This is the one metric where the AI disruption thesis has visible financial traction. Whether this is (a) clients hesitating before renewing/expanding due to AI uncertainty, (b) normal lumpiness in a low-volume metric, or (c) early evidence of structural share loss is the central question for Pass 2. It will be the focus of the earnings call analysis.

**OCF Seasonality Note:** Quarterly OCF is dramatically seasonal. The September quarter (Q1 FY) generates most of the cash ($700M in Q1 FY2026), while the December and March quarters are much weaker. The TTM FCF of $1.30B is the right number to use; quarterly snapshots are misleading.

**Operating Leverage — Strong at group level, weak in ICS.**
5yr average operating leverage: 2.16x (healthy). Most recent nine months in ICS: roughly 0.2x (revenue +9%, EBT +2%). GTO nine months: ~4.1x (EBT +37% on +9% revenue). ICS is the concern. However, ICS operating leverage is depressed by acquisition costs and the postage pass-through (which inflates revenue without a proportional margin). Normalize for postage and ICS leverage looks better.

**Capex / D&A — Asset-light with high intangible amortization.**
Capex/D&A: 21% TTM — extremely light. Capex is $71.5M TTM against D&A of $339.9M. The large D&A is driven by amortization of acquired intangibles ($52.8M per quarter per the non-GAAP reconciliation, or ~$211M annualized), not by physical asset depreciation. This is consistent with a serial acquirer of software businesses. Maintenance capex appears minimal; the business does not need to reinvest heavily to sustain its earnings power.

**Debt Profile — Steadily improving.**
Debt/Assets: 51.6% (2021) → 36.8% (current) — consistent deleveraging. Debt/OCF: 6.55x (2021) → 2.36x (current). The balance sheet is in very good shape. No near-term refinancing risk or covenant pressure.

**SBC — Benign.**
SBC/Revenue: ~1.1% consistently. Well below software industry norms (3-5%). Q3 FY2026 SBC rose to $30.8M (1.6% of revenue, vs 0.8% in prior quarter) — a jump worth watching but not alarming in isolation.

#### B. Targeted Searches (Items Flagged from Financials)

**1. What are the digital asset gains?**
The nine-month digital asset gain of $2.03/share pre-tax appears in the non-GAAP reconciliation as "Gains or Losses on Digital Assets." This is excluded from Adjusted EPS. In GTO's capital markets product line, the MD&A notes "2pts from digital asset revenues" as a positive factor in internal growth. This suggests Broadridge holds or processes digital assets (likely cryptocurrency-related trading infrastructure) and recorded mark-to-market or realized gains during the crypto market surge in H1 FY2026 (calendar Q3/Q4 2025). This is non-recurring by nature and management explicitly excludes it from adjusted metrics.

**2. ICS margin pressure — is it structural?**
ICS nine months: revenue +9%, EBT +2%. The cost breakdown: distribution expenses +10% (primarily $91M of postage increases), volume-related expenses and acquisition costs. The postage pass-through dilutes reported margins but is largely neutral at the gross level. However, labor and technology investments are compressing operating profit beyond just postage. Per the MD&A, cost of revenues in Q3 grew $90.8M (+7%) while SG&A grew $36.5M (+16% — largely acquisition and compensation-related). The margin compression is real but the drivers are: (a) postage pass-through (pass-through economics), (b) deliberate investment in acquisitions and headcount. Neither is evidence of the core moat eroding.

**3. Why are closed sales declining?**
The filings give no explicit explanation for the -16-19% closed sales decline. This is the most important open question for Pass 2.

#### C. Accounting Checklist (Independent, Mandatory)

**1. Revenue Recognition**
Broadridge's revenue follows standard ASC 606 patterns for a subscription/services business. Recurring revenues are recognized ratably over contract terms. Event-driven revenues are recognized upon processing events. Distribution is recognized when services are rendered. No unusual revenue recognition policies identified. The company separates recurring vs. event-driven vs. distribution revenue in its segment disclosures, giving good visibility. Deferred revenue and contract assets are disclosed but not flagged as unusual in scale. No concerns.

**2. Expense Recognition & Cost Capitalization**
Capitalized software development costs are included within D&A and disclosed. Amortization of acquired intangibles is large (~$211M annualized) but properly disclosed and clearly excluded from adjusted metrics. Restructuring costs are disclosed and appropriately separated ($20M one-time facility closure, completed in Q3 FY2026). No concerns about expense misclassification.

**3. Balance Sheet & Asset Valuation**
Goodwill: The company performs annual impairment testing. Per the critical accounting estimates section, a 10% adverse change in projected OCF, discount rates, or terminal value growth rates would not trigger impairment. The goodwill balance is likely substantial given serial acquisitions (Acolin, iJoin, Signal, SIS among recent ones), but impairment risk appears low given underlying FCF growth. No Level 3 fair value concerns flagged in filings. No off-balance sheet arrangements of note (Note 19 references standard operating leases and contractual obligations). No related-party transactions flagged.

**4. Cash Flow & Working Capital**
Working capital is structurally negative and highly volatile (CV: 2.45) due to seasonality and the pass-through nature of distribution revenues. This is not a quality concern — it reflects advance client payments and is characteristic of the business model. OCF/NI at 1.24x TTM confirms earnings-to-cash conversion is healthy. The dramatic quarterly swings in OCF (1.87x in Q1, 0.26x in Q2) are structural seasonality, not a red flag.

**5. Non-GAAP Metrics & Adjusted Earnings**
The non-GAAP framework is clearly disclosed and reasonable. Add-backs: amortization of acquired intangibles (~$211M annualized), acquisition/integration costs (~$19M annualized), restructuring (~$18M), and digital asset gains/losses. The digital asset gain exclusion is appropriate and disclosed. SBC is NOT added back (unlike many tech companies) — this makes Broadridge's adjusted EPS more conservative than peers. The GAAP P/E (16.3x) < Adj P/E (17.1x) anomaly is fully explained by the digital asset gain inflating GAAP earnings above adjusted earnings in the current TTM window. No concerns about aggressive non-GAAP methodology.

**Data quality flag for tracker:** The -9.5% earnings miss recorded in our system for 2026-04-30 ($2.38 reported vs $2.63 estimate) appears to compare GAAP EPS to an estimate set on an adjusted basis. Adjusted Q3 EPS was $2.72 — a beat of ~$0.09 (+3.4%) vs the $2.63 estimate. Our earnings script may have a GAAP/adjusted alignment issue for this ticker.
