# Fair Isaac (FICO) — Q3 FY2026 Breakdown

## Synthesis and Takeaways

- **The quality and the moat are elite and not really in question; the durability of the growth *rate* is the whole debate.** Consolidated GAAP operating margin is 51.7%, free cash flow is roughly 42% of revenue, and return on invested capital is 76.5% and rising every year for five years (FICO_financial_analysis.md). The profit is genuinely backed by cash — operating cash flow runs 1.23x net income and capex is essentially zero — so there is no soft adjusted number propping up a worse one (FICO_financial_analysis.md; 10-Q).
- **Growth is fast in level but decelerating, and it is almost entirely price, not volume.** Total revenue grew +26% in Q3 (from +39% in Q2), the Scores engine grew +41%, and mortgage-origination revenue specifically grew +97% — yet mortgage *volumes* grew only low-single-digit (Q3 2026 call). The entire Scores growth story is unit price on flat volume, which is powerful today and the source of both the competitive and regulatory pressure.
- **The mortgage moat looks genuinely durable, and management can quantify why.** On the GSEs' own released data, Milliman found FICO 10T beats VantageScore 4 on all three predictiveness measures using the *same* underlying data, so the edge is modeling, not data access (Q3 2026 call). VantageScore's conforming-mortgage share is structurally capped "in the 20s," is ~2% outside conforming mortgage, and is 0% in nonconforming, and the MBS market has securitized only ~20 loans ever on Vantage paper (Q2/Q3 2026 call Q&A).
- **The threat is real but contained and partly self-inflicted.** VantageScore "gaming" (lenders pulling both scores and submitting the better one) is confirmed at Rocket and UWM, but it requires FICO to be pulled too, so it is additive to FICO's count, not volume loss (Q3 2026 call Q&A). The bigger structural risks are dependence and durability of pricing: the three bureaus that co-own VantageScore are now 63% of revenue, and a Florida antitrust probe is targeting exactly the pricing power that drove the +97% (news, 2026-07-04).
- **The balance sheet is the one place quality and safety diverge.** Unlike a fortress software peer, FICO deliberately levered up to buy back stock: $5.58B debt at a 5.64% blended rate against $305M cash, negative equity, 5.6x debt/OCF, a covenant stepping to 3.5x, and a ~$2.4B maturity wall in 2028 (Q3 2026 call; 10-Q notes). FCF covers it comfortably, but it removes the margin for error.
- **The stock got much cheaper while earnings rose, and the next catalyst is binary and unscheduled.** It trades near 27x adjusted earnings (~31x GAAP) versus a five-year median above 50x, and is down about 46% from its 52-week high near $1,998 (news, 2026-06-08; market data). The binary lever is the Direct License Program (DLP) / FICO 10T success-fee model, which is live-pending one GSE certification, and the ~Nov 4 print (Q4 FY2026) is the next read on whether the price-only growth engine holds.

Put together, this looks like paying about 70 to 75 cents for a dollar of value. The economics, the returns, and the mortgage moat are elite and the derating is real, but the growth is now a pure pricing story on flat volume, the catalyst that re-accelerates it (DLP) has already slipped past management's own timeline, and the same pricing power that drives the numbers is what draws the antitrust and bureau-conflict fire — so it is a strong risk/reward, not a slam-dunk bargain.

## Growth

The headline is strong in level, decelerating in trend, and — unlike most software — driven almost entirely by price rather than volume or new products.

The engine is the Scores segment, which is 68% of revenue and threw off +41% growth in Q3 at a 91% segment margin (Q3 2026 call). Inside that, mortgage-origination revenue grew +97% year over year and is 62% of all Scores revenue, while mortgage *volumes* grew only low-single-digit, "consistent with the bureaus" (Q3 2026 call; Q3 2026 call Q&A). So essentially all of the growth is a higher per-score unit price, not more scores. That is the defining feature of the business and the crux of the debate: it is extraordinarily high-margin and cash-generative, but it is a pricing story with a ceiling, on a franchise that already has ~90% B2B share.

The trend is decelerating, and it is important to read it at the right level. Total revenue can be lumpy quarter to quarter because Software carries point-in-time license revenue, so the cleaner signal is the Scores/mortgage line:

- Scores segment YoY: **+60% (Q2 FY2026) → +41% (Q3 FY2026)**
- Mortgage-origination revenue YoY: **+127% (Q2) → +97% (Q3)**
- Total revenue YoY: **+39% (Q2) → +26% (Q3)**; full-year guidance **+20%** (Q2/Q3 2026 calls)

A key correction on that deceleration: it is comp- and rate-driven, not share loss. Management was explicit that Q2's +127% was inflated by prior-year pricing timing and a strong-volume quarter, and that Q3 slowed because "as rates tick up, volumes slow down" — the same softness the bureaus reported (Q3 2026 call Q&A). There is no evidence of FICO losing scores to VantageScore in the numbers; the slowdown is the mortgage market, plus a hard comp.

The forward growth story has two legs beyond raising the mortgage rack rate. The first is the **DLP / FICO 10T success-fee model**: FICO cut the upfront price to $0.99 per score and added a $65 funding fee per closed loan (revised down from an initial $4.95 + $33), a model designed to match VantageScore on entry price while monetizing on funded loans instead (Q2 2026 call). It is revenue-neutral versus the per-score model by management's own framing, is signed with resellers covering ~60% of mortgage volume (path to ~90%), and is live-pending one GSE certification — a real but repeatedly delayed catalyst (Q3 2026 call). The second is **Software/Platform**: reported Software grew only +2%, but that masks a healthy transition — Platform revenue grew +66%, Platform ARR grew +62% to $413M and crossed non-platform for the first time, and Platform net retention is 148% (Q3 2026 call). The offset is that legacy non-platform is running off (−17% ARR), holding blended Software growth down and compressing Software margin.

Notably, FICO gave **no FY2027 guidance** and did not reset a long-term growth rate. The durable question the numbers cannot yet answer is whether price (via DLP/10T) can keep the Scores engine growing once the mortgage repricing cycle matures, given volumes are essentially flat.

## Profitability

This is the strongest and least disputed part of the story, and it is not a recent or fragile phenomenon.

- **Margins are elite, stable, and structurally rising.** Consolidated GAAP operating margin is 51.7% TTM (53.8% in Q3), up every year from 38.4% in 2021, with very low variability (CV 0.08) (FICO_financial_analysis.md). Non-GAAP operating margin was 62% in Q3 (Q3 2026 call). The Scores segment runs a 91% operating margin; Software runs 26% (down from 32%). Because Scores is now 68% of revenue, the mix shift alone mechanically lifts the consolidated margin — which is the tell that the margin story is Scores-dependent.
- **Free cash flow is enormous and real.** TTM FCF is ~$1.0B, about 42% of revenue; the company frames it as $961M over the last four quarters, up 28% (FICO_financial_analysis.md; Q3 2026 call). Capex is negligible (~$5.5M TTM, 0.2% of revenue), so FCF ≈ operating cash flow — a defining feature of the capital-light Scores model (FICO_financial_analysis.md).
- **Earnings quality is high.** Operating cash flow is 1.23x net income on a stable five-year basis, stock-based comp is only 7.3% of revenue and *declining*, and there are no unusual add-backs — the GAAP-to-adjusted EPS gap (~13-14%) is essentially just SBC (FICO_financial_analysis.md; 10-Q). The one wobble — Q2's 0.84x OCF/NI — was a receivables-timing bulge from the pricing surge that fully reversed to 1.60x in Q3 (FICO_financial_analysis.md).
- **Returns on capital are exceptional and widening.** ROIC is 76.5% TTM, up from 41% in 2021 (FICO_financial_analysis.md). Part of that is a mechanically small invested-capital base (buybacks pushed equity negative), but even on a conservative basis the franchise earns far above any cost of capital — the moat shows up directly in the returns, and the direction is up.
- For scale, versus PTC (a strong software peer used as a yardstick): FICO's 51.7% operating margin and 76.5% ROIC dwarf PTC's 37.8% and 26.7%, reflecting FICO's near-zero-COGS Scores model (FICO_financial_analysis.md).

Two honest caveats. First, the published 91%/26% segment margins are *pre-SBC and pre-corporate*, so the 51.7% consolidated GAAP figure is the economically representative one, not the segment number (10-Q segment note). Second, and unlike a fortress-balance-sheet peer, this profitability sits on a deliberately levered balance sheet ($5.58B debt, negative equity from buybacks), so "profitable" and "financially unconstrained" are not the same thing here — see Risk.

## Risk

The economics are not the risk. The risks are the durability of the pricing-led growth, structural dependence, regulation aimed squarely at the pricing power, and a levered balance sheet.

- **The growth engine is price on flat volume — and price is what everyone is attacking.** With mortgage volumes flat and the franchise at ~90% share, further growth means charging more per score, which is precisely what the **Florida AG antitrust probe** targets: a civil investigative demand alleging predatory pricing, bundling, and exclusionary bureau contracts, noting FICO's price rose "from under $1 to as much as $10 per score" (news, 2026-07-04). Senator Hawley and a prior B2B antitrust class action are part of the same overhang. This is the most direct threat to the thesis, because it caps the one lever that is actually driving growth.
- **The bureaus are both the distribution channel and the competitor's owners, and the dependence is deepening.** Experian, TransUnion, and Equifax generated 63% of Q3 revenue, up from 54% a year ago, and all three individually exceed 10% (10-Q). They co-own VantageScore. The better FICO's mortgage pricing does, the greater their incentive to push the alternative they own — a structural conflict that intensifies with every price increase (Q3 2026 call Q&A).
- **VantageScore is a contained competitor, not a scale threat — but it is the clearest external warning.** VantageScore 4.0 was in ~30% of Q2 mortgage credit *pulls* per TransUnion (news, 2026-07-31), which sounds alarming, but management's read is that this is gaming (both scores pulled), which is additive to FICO, not substitutive, and the conforming ceiling is structurally "in the 20s" (Q3 2026 call Q&A). Outside conforming mortgage, VantageScore is ~2% and largely unpaid-for (bundled free by bureaus); in nonconforming it is 0%; and the MBS market has securitized ~20 loans ever on Vantage paper (Q2 2026 call Q&A). The durability case rests on FICO 10T being more predictive on the same data and on Vantage's data only going back to 2013 — never tested through a full credit cycle, so MBS investors demand an uncertainty premium (Q2 2026 call Q&A).
- **AI is a tail risk by vertical, not a near-term disruptor.** Fair-lending law, explainability requirements (FICO's 32 reason codes), the redlining prohibition, and the CFPB's shutdown of Upstart's black-box model all constrain AI-native underwriting in regulated credit — and FICO holds 137 AI/explainability patents (Q2 2026 call Q&A). The moat is strongest in mortgage and weakest in personal loans/credit card, where fintech lenders use FICO more for safe-harbor and AI-native models are genuinely competitive.
- **DLP execution keeps slipping.** The success-fee catalyst has been "closing in on it" for multiple quarters; management candidly admitted "this much time…is more than we expected; we actually believed it would be up and running by now" (Q2 2026 call Q&A). It is real but unscheduled, and a push into FY2027 leaves the stock waiting.
- **The balance sheet removes the margin for error.** $5.58B debt at 5.64%, $305M cash, 5.6x debt/OCF, negative equity, a covenant stepping down to 3.5x, and a ~$2.4B maturity wall in 2028 ($1.5B term loan + $900M notes) (Q3 2026 call; 10-Q notes). FCF covers interest several times over and management has shifted to near-term debt paydown over buybacks (Q3 2026 call Q&A), but an FCF shortfall would bite faster than at an unlevered peer.
- **Sentiment is cooling at the margin.** Wolfe downgraded to Peer Perform on the Q3 revenue miss (news, 2026-08-05), average price targets have been marked down over the year, and a director sold ~two-thirds of her stake under a 10b5-1 plan (news, 2026-08-05) — small, but part of a more cautious tape.

## Narrative

FICO spent the last year being repriced on fear rather than results, and the debate has narrowed to a single axis: can a pricing-led growth engine keep going. The business is a ~90%-share credit-scoring franchise with a 91% segment margin and 76.5% ROIC, wrapped around a fast-growing AI-decisioning Software platform. For most of 2025 the market paid above 50x earnings for that. Then the mortgage repricing cycle drove extraordinary Scores growth (+127% mortgage revenue in Q2), the stock ran, and the multiple has since compressed by nearly half — to ~27x — even as earnings kept climbing (news, 2026-06-08). Earnings compounded ~26% a year over five years while the multiple derated; the two forces roughly cancel, which is why the stock sits near its five-year *average price* while the business is materially larger.

Then came the Q3 wobble. Revenue missed consensus by less than 1% while EPS beat and guidance was raised, but the market focused on deceleration — mortgage revenue growth slowing from +127% to +97% and total from +39% to +26% — and Wolfe downgraded on competitive-pressure concerns (news, 2026-08-05). Management's explanation was credible and specific: volumes are flat because rates ticked up, the deceleration is a hard comp plus the mortgage market, and there is no evidence of share loss in the audited numbers (Q3 2026 call Q&A). That reassures on the moat but confirms the bear's real point — the growth is price, not volume, so it depends on continuing to charge more, or on the DLP success-fee model going live.

The competitive and regulatory backdrop sharpened the same story. VantageScore's presence in ~30% of Q2 mortgage *pulls* (news, 2026-07-31) reads as a threat until you see it is gaming that requires FICO to be pulled too, and that the GSEs' own released data — analyzed independently by Milliman — shows FICO 10T is more predictive on all three measures using the same inputs (Q3 2026 call). Meanwhile a Florida antitrust probe went after the pricing power directly (news, 2026-07-04), and the bureaus that distribute FICO and co-own VantageScore are now 63% of revenue — the deepening dependence that is the real structural risk.

Sentiment is split, and that split is the setup. Value-oriented buyers and most analysts see a cheap, elite compounder — Seeking Alpha ran "This Compounder Is On Sale" (June 17) and "Strong Earnings Growth Makes The Valuation Attractive Again" (June 24), and credible long-duration institutions (Northwestern Mutual, Akre, Capital Research, Norges) have been accumulating (news). On the other side, the sell-side is quietly cooling, targets have been marked down over the year, and the DLP catalyst has already slipped past management's own timeline. The whole debate compresses into the next print: around November 4, Q4 FY2026 will show whether flat volumes plus DLP can sustain the price-led engine, or whether the mortgage repricing cycle is maturing without its next monetization lever live. Given elite economics and a durable mortgage moat on one side, and pricing-ceiling, dependence, regulatory, and leverage risks on the other, that print is the near-term swing factor for the thesis.
