### SONY — $21.89

**Business Quality**

Sony's reported numbers are entirely misleading and should be discarded before doing any analysis. The SFGI deconsolidation created a non-cash net loss that voids the TTM EPS, the GAAP P/E is null, and the five-year EPS trend looks like a collapsing business when it isn't. The correct starting points are EV/OI of 11.8x on ¥1,600B ($10.67B) of guided continuing-operations income, and P/FCF of 12.7x on ¥1,530B ($10.2B) of TTM free cash flow. Both are compressed multiples for what the underlying businesses actually are.

The core of Sony is three franchises that have almost nothing to do with each other: the image sensor unit (I&SS) with roughly 50% global CMOS market share and 17.4% OI margins; Music at 21.1% OI margin with decades of streaming royalty duration and 15% annual revenue growth; and PlayStation Network, which generates growing recurring digital revenue on a fixed installed base. Each of these would trade at a significant premium as a standalone. Together, they get blended with a television business in structural decline and a Pictures segment rebuilding after the Pixomondo shutdown, and the whole thing gets discounted as a consumer electronics company.

The balance sheet confirms the underlying quality: Sony went from net debt of ¥589.7B to net cash of +¥539.2B in two years, funded a ¥500B buyback, raised the dividend from ¥25 to ¥35, and did all of it from OCF with no external capital. The ¥466B OCF guidance decline for FY2026 is music catalog M&A classified as operating outflows under IFRS — not business deterioration. Operating income grew +13% on +4% revenue in FY2025, confirming the mix is improving toward higher-quality revenue even as total growth looks modest.

**Valuation**

The price is down 17.4% over the past year while continuing-operations OI grew +13%. That gap compresses the multiple in the right direction — you are paying less for a business that earned more. At $21.89, the stock is in the bottom 23rd percentile of its 1-year range, having bottomed at $20.09 in April 2026. EV/OI at 11.8x and P/FCF at 12.7x are the honest anchors, and both are low for a franchise business with a net cash balance sheet and an 18.8% five-year FCF CAGR.

The I&SS segment alone — ASP rising alongside volume, Q4 FY2026 OI guided to ¥400B annualized — if valued at a comparable sensor-hardware multiple, would represent a meaningful fraction of the entire current market cap. The conglomerate discount is real and may be permanent, but it is unusually wide right now because the dominant narrative does not match the actual earnings trajectory.

**Narrative**

The market treats Sony as a PS5 hardware cycle play. English-language institutional coverage is nearly absent: one stale Bernstein target ($30, downgraded to Market Perform), zero new targets in 90 days. The news flow since March is dominated by UK PS5 sales down 50% after the second price hike and the Afeela EV cancellation — legitimately bad for one segment of five, but the narrative extrapolates it to the whole business.

Reddit is doing better work than the professionals here: r/ValueInvesting threads correctly identify the sensor, music, and gaming franchise value while providing the only clean-eyed counter — that even if I&SS is great, it will keep getting discounted by the conglomerate structure. That is the structurally accurate risk frame. The AI sensing angle — Sony's TSMC JV for automotive ADAS, robotics, and edge AI — doesn't exist in English-language coverage at all. The gap between narrative and financial reality is wide, one-sided, and hasn't begun to close. The narrative is at the floor: early value recognition accumulating at retail quality, no institutional engagement, no event visible yet that changes the category.

**Catalyst — next 3 months**

August 6 is the first clean post-SFGI quarter. The CFO made two specific, falsifiable commitments on the Q4 call: G&NS underlying growth is "steady double-digit," and I&SS OI is tracking toward ¥400B annual — meaning Q1 should print at least ¥95–100B to be on pace. If both hold, the two most important management assertions become empirically supported and the "prove it" overhang lifts.

What won't happen at this print: institutional re-rating. Sony is a $129B market cap. The specific condition that would attract the institutional bid needed for a real re-rating is the TSMC JV binding agreement — financial terms disclosed, capex commitment quantified — which reframes the I&SS narrative from "smartphone sensor maker at a discount" to "AI sensing platform with leading-edge TSMC access." That event is at MOU stage only as of June 2026.

The most important thing to watch August 6: I&SS Q1 OI at or above ¥95B confirms the ¥400B annual pace; below ¥85B is a direct falsification of the CFO's primary commitment.

On the expensive side, OCF at $124B growing at 19% five-year CAGR is the cash machine that matters, and that machine is not adequately captured by the P/E.

The advertising business is genuinely exceptional and the financials prove it with real cash. Revenue accelerated from +22% in FY2025 to +33% in Q1 2026 because ad impressions grew +19% and price per ad grew +12%, both re-accelerating, both AI-driven, both already in the income statement. The 3.56B daily active people have no viable competitor at scale. ROIC at 23.4% confirms above-cost returns — but the trajectory is compressing as the $75.75B TTM capex base inflates invested capital faster than NOPAT, and this continues through the $125–145B 2026 program.

The one open-ended risk is the scale of the infrastructure commitment with no stated ceiling. Management declined to guide 2027 capex on the Q1 2026 call. FCF approaches zero this year — a voluntary infrastructure decision, not deterioration — but "voluntary" with no endpoint is what the market is correctly pricing as a risk.

**Valuation**

The price is down 14.7% over the past year while EPS grew +62.4% (adjusted for opposing tax anomalies). The Spread of -77.1% — earnings outrunning price by 77 percentage points — is the strongest absolute signal in this comparative dataset. You are paying less for a business that earned materially more.

The honest multiple is 58x P/OE. That is not cheap in isolation, but the advertising compounding loop (AI improvements → better ROI → more advertiser spend → more data → better models) is already demonstrably operating in reported financials, not in projections. The valuation question is whether that loop continues at its current rate. If the capex generates returns, the P/OE compresses rapidly. If not, 58x is expensive.

**Narrative**

The dominant narrative is a capex-ROI standoff. The advertising business's quality is not in dispute — the debate is entirely about whether $135B annually of infrastructure spend generates adequate return with no defined endpoint. The April 29 guidance raise triggered the selloff from $796 to $593, and the market has been in "prove it" mode since.

The professional community settled at $835 median target with zero analyst actions in 90 days. Reddit at $600 is selective floor-buying without high conviction. The "cheapest Magnificent Seven" framing is forming. The perception gap with Google is the most useful analytical signal: the identical capex investment reads as reckless for Meta and visionary for Google, despite Meta's advertising business already showing the AI improvements in its income statement that Google's does not yet match at the same rate. The $520 52-week low appears to be the sentiment nadir. Recovery is underway, retail-paced, institutional conviction withheld.

**Catalyst — next 3 months**

July 29. Three conditions simultaneously determine whether this is a re-rating event: (1) Q2 revenue at or above $60.5B, confirming the advertising acceleration wasn't a Q1 aberration; (2) FoA operating margins holding at 47%+ rather than deteriorating from Q1's 48%; (3) any language from Susan Li suggesting 2027 capex below 2026 levels — that single sentence converts the FCF compression from open-ended to bounded and is the input missing from the current bull case.

All three together enable the first analyst upgrades toward the $835 median. The primary downside trigger is a Q2 capex guidance raise above $145B.

The most important thing to watch isn't the revenue line — it's whether Susan Li says anything about 2027 capex direction.

### NVDA — $205.10

**Business Quality**

The best business in this dataset. Earnings per share grew 214% last year while the stock moved only 45% — the business is accelerating faster than the price, and has been for five years. The mechanism is simple: NVDA owns no factories. It designs the chips, TSMC and Samsung build them, and NVIDIA collects 64% operating margins on $253 billion in annual revenue with almost no capital required to grow. The cash generation is real — not accounting presentation. Cash generated from operations exceeds reported profit, the balance sheet has $80 billion liquid against $8.5 billion in debt, and annual free cash flow has been growing at 86% a year for five years.

The returns on capital are 82% reported and closer to 117% on the operating business alone. The only structural issue worth flagging: NVDA has been recycling its cash at extraordinary scale into AI startups that "may indirectly" become customers — $18.6 billion in one quarter, up from $17.5 billion for all of the prior year, now 38% of quarterly cash generation. This creates a self-reinforcing loop management doesn't engage with directly, and it inflates reported earnings by billions in unrealized investment gains. The business is genuinely exceptional; this feature just means you need to use non-GAAP earnings as the anchor, not the GAAP headline.

**Valuation**

Earnings grew 214% last year; the stock moved 45%. The gap between those two numbers is where the valuation case lives. At 44x owner earnings (cash profit minus stock compensation), NVDA looks expensive on the surface — but that multiple is compressing fast as earnings compound. The real question is whether today's $205 price produces an adequate return given the demonstrated growth. If earnings growth slows significantly, it doesn't. If it continues, the multiple will look low in retrospect.

The GAAP price-to-earnings of 31x is actually misleading lower than the real multiple of ~38x because unrealized investment gains inflate reported profits. The honest multiple is ~38x on operating earnings, 44x after deducting stock compensation. Analyst consensus is $300 — 46% above today — with no analyst willing to downgrade despite the last three quarters of record earnings all producing a flat-to-down price reaction.

**Narrative**

Every major institution owns this and is bullish — there is no meaningful bear camp in professional research. The consensus story: NVDA is the irreplaceable layer for the largest capital deployment cycle in computing history, and Jensen Huang has been supplying unusually specific ROI evidence to sustain it — Meta's ad performance improved 3.5% on NVIDIA infrastructure, Anthropic's revenue grew 10x, nine gigawatts of Blackwell is deployed and consumed. The "cheap on forward earnings" framing is everywhere (Motley Fool, Truist), and it's not wrong on the numbers — it just requires Rubin to execute on schedule.

The problem is the market's expectations rise with every beat. The stock has dropped after each of the last three consecutive record quarters.
