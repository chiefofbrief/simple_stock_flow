## IPO Scenario Analysis

*Thought exercise. OpenAI confidential S-1 filed May 22, targeting September at ~$850B–$1T. Anthropic confidential S-1 filed June 1, $47B ARR run-rate, targeting October at ~$965B. SpaceX less relevant to enterprise software. 50/50 weighting. For AI Apps, the operative question is whether the IPOs confirm or undermine the enterprise AI adoption narrative. For AI Defensive, the question runs in reverse — AI IPO outcomes may validate or collapse the disruption narrative that drove these stocks down.*

**Best case:** IPOs validate AI monetization at scale — Anthropic profitable at $47B ARR, OpenAI cash-generative at $20B+. Enterprise buyers see confirmed ROI on AI spend and accelerate adoption across every platform. The SaaSpocalypse narrative is buried for good: AI agents are additive to enterprise software, not replacements. CRM's Agentforce thesis gets institutional consensus; NOW's AI Control Tower becomes the infrastructure story of the year. CRM's reported $5B Anthropic stake re-marks materially higher. For the defensive names: Adobe's Firefly revenue contribution accelerates and the re-rating from platform-not-victim can begin. Intuit's TurboTax Live model is confirmed as the winning AI+human approach. The employment disruption fear that drove ADP down fades as AI is shown to be additive, not destructive, to white-collar headcount.

**Worst case:** S-1s reveal thin or negative margins — OpenAI unprofitable until 2030, Anthropic burning compute to generate compute revenue. Enterprise CFOs ask "if the AI companies themselves can't make money on this, why are we paying premiums for AI features?" AI add-on budgets face scrutiny. Agentforce ARR growth slows. Now Assist renewal rates come under question. The premium multiple on AI narrative across the AI Apps compresses. SAP is the exception — nobody stops running their ERP because AI disappointed. For the defensive names, the dynamic reverses: INTU is the one name that benefits — if AI companies are struggling, the "paste your W-2 into Claude" narrative loses its most powerful prop. ADBE faces a mixed signal: disruption fear eases but Firefly's growth story weakens simultaneously. ADP is essentially unchanged: payroll is payroll regardless of whether Anthropic is profitable.

## AI Apps

### SAP — $184.77

**Business Quality**

SAP's business is a switching-cost story. Enterprises spend years embedding their financial and supply-chain operations into SAP's ERP, and replacement is a multi-year project that most simply don't attempt. That stickiness is real: ROIC at 23.1%, improving, on an invested capital base loaded with €29B in goodwill from decades of acquisitions. Cloud ERP Suite grew 28% last year and is approaching 60% of total revenue as legacy software support — which was always going to decline — shrinks predictably. The total revenue growth rate of 6% looks sleepy, but the mix is moving in the right direction. Zero debt. FCF at $8.06B, self-funding a €10B buyback and a dividend increase simultaneously. The SBC cost is modest at 4.6% of revenue — and importantly, SAP includes it in their adjusted profit figures rather than excluding it, making their non-GAAP numbers more conservative than most US tech peers.

In May 2026 SAP announced two acquisitions that extend the AI build-out significantly. Dremio, an Apache Iceberg-native open data lakehouse expected to close in Q3 2026, will unify SAP and non-SAP enterprise data for real-time agentic AI access — expanding Business Data Cloud beyond SAP's own data estate. Prior Labs, whose tabular foundation models predict business outcomes from structured data (payment delays, churn risk, supplier risk, upsell signals), is expected to close Q2/Q3 2026; SAP committed more than €1 billion over four years to scale it as an independent AI research hub with a direct path to productization across the SAP portfolio. Combined with WalkMe ($1.5B, 2023) for digital adoption, SAP has deployed M&A capital across the full enterprise AI stack — data unification, predictive modeling, and workflow adoption.

**Valuation**

The price is down 39.6% over the past year while EPS grew 9.3%. You are buying less of a business that earned more. Owner earnings — free cash flow minus SBC — produce a P/OE of 31.6x at the current price. That is a reasonable multiple for a ROIC-23% franchise with zero debt and a cloud business growing 28%. The market's case against SAP doesn't appear in any financial statement: cloud revenue grew 27% constant currency in Q1, deal margins held, and no metric — churn, pricing, customer count — shows any competitive attack from AI. The price is discounting a disruption that has not arrived.

**Narrative**

The narrative that drove SAP down — AI agents routing around monolithic ERP, making the software obsolete — has no financial support. But that is only half the picture.

The other half is more aggressive than it looks. SAP's ERP data becomes more valuable as enterprises try to use AI for anything operational — AI needs clean, structured business data to do useful work, and SAP sits on decades of it. But SAP isn't a passive data holder waiting for the AI wave to lift it. Joule now covers 35+ solutions with 15 agentic AI agents embedded across the suite. Business Data Cloud connects that data layer directly to AI applications. And the Dremio and Prior Labs acquisitions materially extend the story: Dremio brings non-SAP enterprise data into the same Apache Iceberg lakehouse, making Business Data Cloud the hub for all enterprise data, not just SAP's. Prior Labs builds predictive models on top of that data — churn risk, payment delays, upsell signals — embedded directly into SAP workflows. The story has shifted from "SAP data becomes the AI input" to "SAP is building the full data-to-AI infrastructure layer, including the model technology on top." That is a materially more ambitious narrative than the market's "defensive ERP" framing, and it hasn't reached institutional consensus yet.

The recovery so far is retail-led. Reddit's value investing community, GuruFocus, and independent research have reached the right conclusion. The institutions have not reversed: the JP Morgan downgrade still stands, no analyst published a new target last month, and there is no institutional bid visible. A €215B company doesn't re-rate on Reddit alone. It needs one clean delivery quarter from a management team that pre-warned its H2 was back-loaded.

**Catalyst — next 3 months**

July 23 is a data gate, not a catalyst. Management already acknowledged that March-April Middle East deal misses cannot be recouped in Q2. The number to watch is current cloud backlog (CCB) growth — ≥25% constant currency sustains the thesis; anything below that extends the wait.

The real event is Q3 October, where H2 deal volume either delivers or doesn't. If CCB holds ≥23% and cloud revenue tracks the guidance range, the deceleration narrative loses its last data support and analyst targets converge toward the $228.50 median. That is the re-rating event; everything before it is setup.

### CRM — $185.66

**Business Quality**

Salesforce's moat is switching costs and data accumulation, not pricing power or structural scarcity. Once enterprises build their sales operations around Salesforce, leaving is painful enough that most don't — which shows up in 95% subscription revenue and $33.6B in contractually committed forward demand growing 14%. FCF at $14.66B, compounding at 28.5% over five years, on CapEx of just 1.3% of revenue. The business doesn't need much capital to sustain itself.

The honest valuation anchor is owner earnings: FCF $14.66B minus SBC $3.55B (8.3% of revenue — a real, recurring labor cost) = $11.1B. At a $152.1B market cap, that is 13.7x. ROIC at 12.5% is positive-spread but thin. And Salesforce has made five major acquisitions in five years, several at returns below estimated cost of capital, with "non-recurring" restructuring charges appearing for five straight years. Capital discipline is the legitimate knock on this business.

**Valuation**

The price is down 31.9% while EPS grew 52%. At 13.7x owner earnings, you are buying a 95% subscription software franchise with $33.6B in contractually committed demand at a price that implies something is structurally wrong. Nothing is structurally wrong. The business generates $11.1B in annual owner earnings and is growing. For a business with this cash flow profile, 13.7x is the clearest case of price disconnecting from economic reality in this cohort. And on top of the base business, Agentforce reached $800M standalone ARR (up 169% year-over-year) across 29,000 deals in its first commercial year — with the broader Agentforce and Data 360 platform combined at $2.9B ARR. A new, growing, disclosed revenue line that is additive to the existing franchise, not a replacement for it.

**Narrative**

Salesforce was the face of the SaaSpocalypse. The thesis was that AI agents would replace the work that CRM software organizes — making the category obsolete. The thesis was wrong. The price moved as if it were right.

Here is what is actually happening: Agentforce reached $800M standalone ARR (up 169% YoY) and 29,000 deals in its first commercial year. Salesforce has processed nearly 20 trillion tokens and converted them into more than 2.4 billion agentic work units. OpenAI's Codex uses Salesforce APIs. Anthropic runs its global operations on Salesforce. The agents that were supposed to kill CRM are running on top of it. That is not a talking point — it is a disclosed revenue line with a growth rate. The Qualified acquisition ($1.5B, AI-driven B2B marketing automation) is the most recent capital deployment signal: Salesforce is building toward full agentic go-to-market automation, not defending legacy CRM. Salesforce is transitioning from AI victim to AI platform, and the transition has already started showing up in reported numbers.

The professional community is in "prove it" mode. All 16 analyst actions after the Q1 24% EPS beat were maintains. No upgrades. They believe the story — median target $280 vs. $185 current price — but they're waiting for one more confirming quarter before committing conviction. Reddit worked through the SaaSpocalypse narrative after Q1 results came out; institutions are next.

**Catalyst — next 3 months**

September 2. Two conditions must be true simultaneously for the upgrade cycle to begin: CRPO growth at or above 13% constant currency — management's stated commitment — and Agentforce ARR tracking toward $1.5-1.8B. Both together convert "prove it" institutional posture into conviction and the 16 maintains become upgrades.

One unscheduled possibility: Salesforce holds a reported $5B pre-IPO stake in Anthropic. If the Anthropic S-1 moves toward public registration before September, that position gets a headline valuation entirely independent of any operating result. Speculative on timing, but it is the one event that could move a $152B stock before the scheduled earnings date.

### WDAY — $144.28

**Business Quality**

Workday has an honest financial problem: ROIC at 9.3% sits below its estimated cost of capital. Four AI acquisitions in the past 18 months — Paradox (AI-native talent acquisition), Sana ($1.1B, enterprise knowledge and learning), Flowise (low-code AI agent builder), and Pipedream (integration platform for AI agents) — have inflated the capital base without yet generating returns. Revenue is decelerating to management's guided 12-13%. And SBC at 16% of revenue is the highest in this cohort — $1.58B per year consumed before shareholders see a dollar. Owner earnings = FCF $2.97B minus SBC $1.58B = $1.39B. At $37.8B market cap, that is 27.1x P/OE. That multiple requires the base case to be delivered cleanly.

What the numbers don't capture is the stickiness. 97% gross retention. $28.1B in contracted subscription backlog. Building a global HR system on Workday is a multi-year project, and nobody rebuilds it. The switching cost is not pricing power — it is operational inertia at enterprise scale. One underappreciated financial driver: the SBC/revenue ratio is declining steadily, from 21.6% in FY22 to 16% today. Each point of compression expands owner earnings meaningfully from a low base. That trajectory is a multi-year tailwind the current P/OE doesn't reflect.

**Valuation**

The price is down 42.9% while non-GAAP EPS grew 24%. The dislocation is real. But at 27.1x P/OE on $1.39B in owner earnings, the case is not "deeply mispriced" in the way CRM or SAP are — it is "punished beyond what the operational facts justify." There is a difference. The price embeds a disruption that hasn't materialized; it does not embed a significant discount to intrinsic value. You are paying a reasonable multiple for something the market is treating as broken when it isn't.

**Narrative**

The narrative against Workday has three mutually reinforcing components. First: AI disrupts headcount — less headcount means fewer seats means less revenue, and seat-based pricing is Workday's core model. Second: Aneel Bhusri returning as CEO is a visible signal that something needed fixing. Third: David Duffield, the co-founder, has sold shares at a 106:0 ratio versus purchases over six months. All three are real concerns. None of them appear in the operating data.

The AI picture is genuinely two-sided here. The threat — seat-based pricing eroding as AI reduces headcounts — is structurally real and hasn't gone away. The opportunity — AI ARR approaching $500M at over 200% growth, expansion deals running 50% larger when AI is included — is also real and growing fast. Workday also unveiled Flex Credits in 2026 — a consumption-based subscription model for AI that lets customers pay for AI work done rather than headcounts managed. It is a direct structural response to the seat-pricing bear thesis, not a talking point. The response is partial: Flex Credits extend pricing optionality alongside the core seat model, they don't replace it. But it signals that management understands the structural concern and is building around it. Management explicitly deferred material AI revenue contribution to FY28. The honest answer is that Workday has real AI optionality and is actively addressing the seat-pricing concern — but the structural question is not fully resolved, making this still the most ambiguous AI positioning in the cohort.

Institutional accumulation has started: First Eagle initiated a $556M position, the Q1 beat produced a +19% price response where prior beats had produced nothing. But Goldman cut its target on the day of the beat, zero analysts upgraded, and Duffield's selling hasn't stopped. The narrative is in early transition, not recovery.

**Catalyst — next 3 months**

Two events, neither guaranteed. First: a Financial Analyst Day where management resets FY28 targets — no date has been set, but management committed to holding it, and if it comes before August 20 with targets meaningfully above current consensus, it is the single largest potential re-rating event in this cohort. Second: Q2 August 20, where three specific conditions determine whether the institutional upgrade cycle begins — subscription revenue ≥$2.455B, cRPO re-accelerating above 15%, and AI ARR at $600M+. All three together are the catalyst; two without the third is another beat that doesn't move institutions.

**Expected value: a dollar for 95 cents.** The price decline is real and the disruption thesis is unsupported, but 27.1x P/OE on below-WACC ROIC doesn't offer a compelling margin of safety. The SBC compression story is genuine and underappreciated — but it is a 3-5 year driver, not a 3-month one. The AI positioning is the most ambiguous in the cohort: real optionality on the upside, real structural question on seat pricing on the downside. The most uncertain setup in the group.

### NOW — $114.50

**Business Quality**

ServiceNow has the best operational profile in this cohort, and it is not close. 98% renewal rate. 22% revenue CAGR without a single down year over five years. $28.2B in contractually committed forward demand. FCF margins structurally above 33%, on CapEx of 5.7% of revenue. The moat is platform lock-in at the deepest level of enterprise IT operations: ServiceNow becomes the system through which enterprises track, route, and resolve work across every department. Once it is embedded, it is invisible infrastructure. Nobody replaces invisible infrastructure.

Owner earnings = FCF $4.63B minus SBC $2.03B (14.6% of revenue) = $2.60B. At a $118.1B market cap, that is 45.4x P/OE. That is the tension: the operational metrics are exceptional, but the multiple requires continuous execution without error. The Armis acquisition ($7.75B, completed April 20, 2026) adds roughly $400M in annual debt service and has pushed estimated Debt/OCF from 0.45x to ~1.56x — a balance sheet transformation now on the books, creating 12-18 months of remaining integration risk during exactly the period when the AI monetization story needs to compound. Notably, ServiceNow's security workflow business had already crossed $1B in ACV organically before the acquisition closed — Armis builds on a proven position, not a new bet.

**Valuation**

The price is down 44.4% from a year ago and fell 58% peak-to-trough. Non-GAAP EPS grew 42% over the same period. Zero financial evidence of disruption at any point during the decline: 22% revenue growth, 98% renewal, RPO growing 23-27%. The stock fell on a metaphor.

At 45.4x P/OE, this is not a business trading below intrinsic value in the conventional sense. The price has recovered from an irrational extreme to a demanding premium. The argument is not "cheap" — it is "quality business that was incorrectly priced for disruption and is recovering toward fair value." The 45.4x multiple requires the 22% growth and AI monetization trajectory to continue. If either stumbles, the multiple compresses and the margin of error here is limited.

**Narrative**

ServiceNow went from one of the biggest victims of the AI fear trade to one of its biggest beneficiaries — and the reversal is the most complete in this cohort. The same platform capabilities that the market feared would be automated around turned out to be exactly where AI agents run. Enterprises need something to orchestrate AI agents across their operations. ServiceNow is already the orchestration layer for IT work. Now Assist ACV is tracking toward $1.5B by year-end, with 70% of NOW's AI customers already using it in pre-production or production environments. The "AI victim" narrative has been replaced by the "AI Control Tower" thesis, and that replacement is backed by disclosed metrics, not just analyst framing.

IT practitioners on Reddit figured this out before the institutions did, from direct product experience: "I work in IT consulting and I cannot imagine NOW being replaced by any other tool, it's too embedded into the enterprises." BofA reinstated Buy on the AI Control Tower thesis. Seeking Alpha described the re-rating as "just beginning." This is one of the rare situations where the fear narrative collapsed faster than the financial evidence arrived — the operational data never supported the bear case, and the AI opportunity thesis is now accumulating against a base that was never fundamentally impaired.

With 67 analysts, 39 already at Buy/Outperform, and 87% institutional ownership, NOW corrects mispricings faster than any name in this cohort. The upgrade cycle hasn't started — zero upgrades in 90 days — but the positioning for it is already in place. The question is the Q2 data.

**Catalyst — next 3 months**

The July 22 print resolves one specific thing: subscription gross margin. Q1 came in at 78% — the trough driven by AI inference costs and the hyperscaler transition. Management guided FY2026 at 81.5%, meaning Q1 was supposed to be the low. If Q2 prints ≥81%, the margin recovery is confirmed, the one credible remaining bear argument is removed, and 67 analysts with 39 already at Buy have their empirical trigger to upgrade.

If Q2 prints below 79%, it puts the full-year guidance at risk and reopens the structural cost compression story.

One metric. One question. The clarity of the setup is itself the most attractive feature of the NOW position over the next three months.

## AI Defensive

### ADP — $229.27

EPS has grown at 12.5% annually over five years through operating leverage, with the CFO explicitly signaling margin expansion acceleration and elevated buybacks in FY2027.

The price is down 26.2% while EPS grew 10.5%. At 18.4x owner earnings, this is not deeply cheap — it's a reasonable price for a defensive franchise with the earnings profile ADP has demonstrated, with a fear discount built in. The specific data against it: pays-per-control grew +1% for two consecutive periods in ADP's own national payroll base — no headcount decline visible in the clients ADP actually serves.

**Narrative**

The fear narrative is a single thesis: AI displaces white-collar workers, ADP's per-employee pricing model loses revenue structurally. It drove the stock from $303 to a $203 low while EPS beat four consecutive quarters. ADP's own payroll data is the most direct available measure of the disruption it's supposedly facing, and it shows the opposite. What the market is also missing: ADP is deploying AI offensively — 30 minutes per payroll saved, 80% reduction in Smart Actions clicks, 35% compliance labor reduction — quantified productivity improvements disclosed on the Q3 earnings call for the first time. ### INTU — $299.77

**Business Quality**

Intuit's durable position isn't TurboTax software — it's the regulatory coverage and proprietary tax data that make TurboTax Live (supervised AI + human expert) irreplicable by a general-purpose LLM. That is the part the disruption thesis misses. TurboTax Live is now 53% of TurboTax revenue, after growing customer count 38% and revenue 36% in FY2026. The market is pricing a "paste your W-2 into Claude" scenario; the evidence shows consumers migrating toward the more expensive AI-assisted expert tier, not away from the platform.
