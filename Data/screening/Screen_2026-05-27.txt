PER-TICKER NARRATIVES
──────────────────────────────────────────────

MELI — The spread is negative at -18.2% and the P/E correlation is positive at +0.64, which together
say price and earnings have been moving together but price has fallen harder than earnings — a modest
but real signal. The complication is that EPS is itself down 15.5% YoY, so the spread is driven by
price falling more than EPS fell, not by earnings outpacing price in the conventional sense. FCF tells
a better story: up 91% in one year and 19% over two, against revenue compounding at 49% YoY. ROIC is
unavailable for financial companies with this capital structure — FCF is the right quality lens, and
on that basis MELI is generating real cash at scale. The open question before adding to the tracker:
is the EPS decline a genuine earnings deterioration or a one-time investment cycle (R&D, market
expansion) that the FCF trend contradicts?

ACM — The spread is the strongest clean signal in the batch at -62.3%: price down 34% while EPS grew
29% — a textbook dislocation. P/E correlation at +0.70 shows price and earnings were moving in sync
earlier in the year, making the recent divergence sharper in context. At 17.3x GAAP P/E this is cheap
for a business with 13.8% ROIC recovering strongly (+10.9pp over two years). The serious flag is FCF:
down over 115% in one year and 137% over two while EPS grows — this is the most acute
earnings-quality mismatch in the batch and cannot be absorbed silently. ROIC recovery and EPS growth
are real, but the cash is not materializing. The open question: is the FCF collapse driven by a
specific large project drawdown, working capital investment, or something that impairs the EPS quality
permanently?

NU — The spread is -53.7%, which looks compelling on its face, but NU's price is actually up 10% YoY
— the negative spread is driven entirely by EPS growing much faster (+64%) than price rose. That is
technically the right signal, but the price is not dislocated downward; it is rising. The P/E
correlation near zero means price and earnings have been disconnecting systematically throughout the
year. The quality picture is the worst in the batch: OCF/NI at 0.38x is a serious flag (earnings far
exceed cash generation), FCF is declining across both 1Y and 2Y while EPS compounds. These are not
the characteristics of a durable signal — they suggest earnings quality problems, not temporary price
dislocation. OCF/NI below 0.8 requires explicit explanation before any thesis is viable.

FIS — The spread of -3,104.6% is entirely a base-effect artifact: FIS reported near-zero EPS in the
prior year due to a large impairment, so the current period's recovery looks like a 3,059% EPS gain
against a -46% price move. The ROIC story is real — 14.1% ROIC up 10pp in one year and 36pp over two
years — and reflects genuine operational improvement post-divestiture. FCF is $2.78B and stable. But
no spread, P/E, or YoY EPS figure can be interpreted at face value without normalizing for the
prior-period distortion. The open question: what does a normalized EPS base look like, and does the
spread hold up once it is recalculated against apples-to-apples earnings?

ACN — The strongest clean signal in the batch. Price down 42% in one year and 38% over two — a
persistent, broad dislocation with no acute catalyst distorting the comparison. EPS growing at 3.9%
YoY and 11.4% over two years, with quarterly momentum at +7.1% QoQ — modest but steady, not a
single-quarter spike. The spread of -46.1% reflects a real divergence where price has moved far
further than earnings justify. ROIC at 24.8% is the only above-20% reading in the batch — this is a
business creating durable value above its cost of capital, declining slightly (-1.3pp/-2.4pp) but from
a strong base. FCF has nearly doubled over two years. At 14.5x P/E and 10.5x P/OE the valuation is
cheap relative to quality. The open question: why is a 24.8%-ROIC business down 42%? Understanding
the narrative driving the dislocation (AI displacement fears, federal spending exposure) is the single
most important step before thesis construction.

VALE — The spread is +53.2% with price up 87% in one year — this is the opposite of what the
framework targets. EPS grew 33% but price more than doubled it. ROIC is 7.0% and declining sharply
(-7pp in one year, -9.8pp over two) — a business below its cost of capital and getting worse. FCF
down 68% over two years despite the recent recovery. There is no signal here in the direction the
framework looks for, and quality is deteriorating. The only scenario worth revisiting: if commodity
prices correct and price reverts while earnings hold — but that is speculation, not a signal.

CMCSA — The spread is a modest +8.1% — price fell a little faster than earnings, which themselves
declined. The more interesting signal is in the quarterly trend: EPS QoQ of +32.7% suggests a live
recovery in real time that the annual figure is not yet reflecting. At 5.0x GAAP P/E and 4.7x P/OE
this is the cheapest name in the batch, and the P/OE near P/E indicates SBC is negligible. ROIC at
12.8% improving modestly. FCF declined 25% in one year but is flat over two years — not a clean
deterioration. The open question: is the EPS quarterly recovery driven by a sustainable business
improvement or a timing artifact in a structurally declining cable subscriber base?

M — The spread is +34.9% with price up 86% in one year — Macy's benefited from the same base-effect
distortion as FIS, with prior-year losses making current EPS growth look extraordinary. The underlying
business shows revenue declining on both 1Y (-1.1%) and 2Y (-5.5%) — the top line is contracting.
FCF is $1.06B but down slightly. ROIC at 8.1% is marginal. With price having nearly doubled, the
dislocation signal is fully inverted — price has far outrun any fundamental recovery. There is no case
to make here under the current framework.

DAL — The spread is +285.9%*, entirely base-effect-driven: EPS YoY of -219% reflects prior-year
losses making current earnings look like a collapse on a percentage basis, but the quarterly trend
(+152.4% QoQ) is strongly positive and more informative than the annual figure. Price is up 67% in
one year. The tension is that even if the quarterly trend reflects a genuine operational recovery,
price has already moved substantially ahead. At 11.9x P/E with real earnings and $3.92B FCF growing
modestly, this is not expensive — but the spread is deeply inverted and the dislocation the framework
targets is absent. If the quarterly momentum continues and price corrects, this becomes interesting.
Today it does not clear the bar.
