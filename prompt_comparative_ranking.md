# Prompt: Comparative Ranking — AI Infrastructure

**Task:** Rank all AI Infrastructure companies in `Data/comparative/Comparative_Analysis.md` by 3-month risk/reward and provide a purchase recommendation.

---

## What to read

1. `Data/comparative/Comparative_Analysis.md` — the full AI Infrastructure section (Tables 1 and 2, all metric flags, all company write-ups). This is your primary source. Do not skim it.
2. `Data/comparative/Comparative_Summary_AI.md` — for the ratings table. Use it as a cross-reference, not as a substitute for the full analysis.

Do not rank based on the summaries alone. The ranking must be grounded in the full comparative analysis.

---

## What to produce

### 1. Ranked table

Rank all 11 companies (SONY, META, MSFT, TSM, GEV, ASML, NVDA, COHR, KLAC, GOOG, FCX) from most to least attractive for a purchase decision over the next 3 months.

Format:

```
Rank | Ticker | Expected Value     | Quality | Valuation | Narrative | Catalyst | One-line rationale
1    | XXX    | dollar for $X      | X       | X         | X         | X        | [why it tops the list]
...
```

### 2. Rationale by tier

Group the ranked companies into tiers and explain each tier in plain language:

- **Buy now** — strong expected value, specific near-term catalyst, risk/reward clearly positive
- **Watch** — good business, but catalyst uncertain or valuation stretched; wait for a specific condition
- **Avoid / Wrong time** — either the price is too high for what you're getting, or the 3-month setup is unfavorable regardless of long-term quality

For each tier, explain what the companies in it have in common and what distinguishes them.

### 3. The trade-offs

Name the 2–3 most interesting decision-making tensions across the full set — places where two companies are close but one ranks higher for a specific reason. These are the decisions that actually require judgment.

---

## Standards

- 3-month horizon is primary. Longer-term quality matters only insofar as it affects the 3-month risk/reward.
- Valuation must be assessed relative to demonstrated earnings growth, not in isolation. A high multiple on a fast-growing business may be more attractive than a low multiple on a stagnating one.
- All claims must trace back to the comparative analysis. No invented data.
- Plain language. The output should read like a smart analyst telling you where to put your money, not a presentation deck.
- If two companies are genuinely too close to separate confidently, say so. False precision is worse than acknowledged uncertainty.
