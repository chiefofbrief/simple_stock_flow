## Q&A

Corporate Strategy and Earnings Announcement Presentation
Q&A (Summary)
Date: May 8, 2026 (Fri)
IMPORTANT: PLEASE READ THE FOLLOWING STATEMENT
For your reference, below please find an English summary of the question and answer session for the
Corporate Strategy and Earnings Announcement Presentation, which was conducted in Japanese.
This English summary, which is intended to replace the simultaneous translation of the question and
answer session previously provided, is not intended to be a direct translation of the question and
answer session. As a result, there may be some differences between this English summary and the
simultaneous English interpretation provided at the question and answer session.

Questioner 1
Q: [I&SS Segment] Regarding the establishment of the joint venture with TSMC, what led Sony to
agree to a memorandum of understanding (MOU) as the controlling shareholder among the various
options?
A: With regard to the I&SS business, what we have consistently stated is that, in order to improve
profitability while keeping capital expenditure burdens under control, various structural innovations
are necessary. Last fiscal year, I stated that we wanted to pursue a fab-light strategy. The joint
venture with TSMC can be described as the first step in that direction. Until now, we have operated
in an IDM (Integrated Device Manufacturer)-like model, handling everything from R&D to the
manufacturing of image sensors in-house. Looking ahead, however, we would like to consider the
possibility of manufacturing not only by ourselves, but also together with partners. It is in this context
that we reached an agreement on this MOU with TSMC. This move should be understood as being
consistent with what we have been communicating since last year.
Q: [Consolidated] About the recent share price. Due to soaring semiconductor memory prices and
the perception that Sony is not an AI-related stock, the share price has continued to hit year-to-date
lows in recent days. While it turned positive temporarily after today’s earnings announcement, the
market reaction suggests that these concerns have not been fully dispelled. Based on your
explanation of Sony’s corporate strategy, do you believe you were able to provide the market with a
sufficient explanation? 
A: Potential factors that growth cannot be achieved as expected are attributable to the memory
shortages and that our cost structure could be significantly impaired. However, those issues not only
affect us, but also affect the sector as a whole. In addition, there may be concerns that as AI evolves
more broadly, content creation in the entertainment industry will become easier across the board,
leading to an increase in the volume of content and intensified competition for consumers’ time,
potentially preventing the entertainment industry itself from enjoying the same level of growth as
before. In today’s presentation, we explained our current stance, our initiatives, and the results we
have achieved so far in response to these concerns. AI itself will continue to evolve rapidly, and new
business models will likely emerge. It is important that we respond flexibly to these changes. While
market perceptions are not something we alone can change, as we have done consistently, we intend
to enhance both the granularity and quality of the information we provide in order to promote the
market’s understanding.
Questioner 2
Q: [I&SS Segment] Regarding the JV with TSMC, the understanding to date has been that Sony
handles the pixel technology, while logic is produced by foundries such as TSMC, with Sony
performing the final integration of image sensors. Your refinement of pixel technology has been
viewed as a key differentiator. In addition, image sensors have not always required aggressive
miniaturization, and the difficulty of analog technology has been a barrier to entry for competitors.
Is there any concern that, by forming a JV, refinement of pixel technology will no longer be handled
solely in-house? What is the primary expectation for the JV?
A: Your understanding that pixels have been handled by Sony and logic by TSMC is correct. With
respect to pixels, we recognize that there are distinct elements, namely development capabilities and
process technology. Up until now, we have vertically integrated these elements in an IDM-like
manner. What will be strengthened through the JV with TSMC is the opportunity to work with a
company that possesses world-leading semiconductor process technology, which we expect to
represent a significant evolution for us. Another important point is the potential to secure future
expansion as a result. Until now, our ability to supply image sensors has been constrained by the
production capacity of our own facilities. Going forward, in addition to mobile sensors, sensors are
likely to play a major role in what is often referred to as “physical AI.” From the perspective of

Questioner 3
Q: [G&NS Segment] Regarding the impact of memory shortages on game console-related businesses,
console prices, including those of other companies, are rising, which may be affecting their adoption.
While the PS5, currently in its adoption phase, may be relatively resilient, could you explain how
console pricing over the next one to three years, including the next-generation platforms, could impact
the console-related business?
A: Rising memory prices increase bill of materials (BOM) costs and worsen manufacturing costs. As
you noted, if these increases are passed on through pricing, they could have a significant impact on
console adoption. That said, we have already secured the memory volumes necessary for 2026, and
pricing has been agreed on to a certain extent. With respect to future changes in memory prices, we
aim to respond by balancing factors such as hardware pricing and the level of promotional spending.
These costs have already been incorporated into our FY2026 forecast. As for next-generation
platforms, no decisions have been made regarding timing or pricing, and we plan to consider these
matters while monitoring future conditions. At present, the prevailing view is that memory prices
will remain elevated and supply-demand conditions will remain tight through 2027. Based on this
assumption, we aim to carefully consider what options are available to us. We intend to examine
every assumption and simulation, including how much we can reduce hardware costs in other areas,
what kinds of sales and go-to-market strategies we can deploy, and even potential business model
adjustments, in order to identify the optimal solution. At the same time, we currently have 125
million monthly active users enjoying games on our platform, and this number has continued to grow.
Taking into account that demand has not disappeared, we believe there are ways to navigate this
environment.

Q: [All Other] Although the development and launch of AFEELA of Sony Honda Mobility has been

A: Against the backdrop of changes in how cars are built in the context of SDV (Software-Defined
Vehicles), our original motivation was to explore how Sony can contribute. Based on the hypothesis
that autonomous driving would become possible and that the in-car space would gain value as an

Questioner 5
Q: [G&NS Segment] Please explain where you see the upside for long-term growth in the G&NS
segment.
A: From a long-term perspective, I believe that game content requires evolution, and there is sufficient
potential for AI to drive that. The game industry is quite mature, and in reality, market share is
dependent on major publishers’ large-scale franchise titles. The emergence of Fortnite, a major live
game service title, before COVID-19 was innovative and acted as a tailwind for the entire industry,
and similar innovation is important for the entertainment market. As development of large-scale
franchise titles becomes more costly and time-consuming, which makes it harder to take risks, I think
AI can lower the barriers to development and increase the likelihood of new titles emerging, and I
expect that to lead to the revitalization of the entire industry. While there are concerns that this
could lead to disruption, we believe such innovation will lead to market expansion, and capturing
these opportunities is what we consider to be the most important initiative.
Q: [Music Segment] Amid risks of AI-generated music, do you see any impact on the valuation of
music catalogs in the market? Also, please explain your thoughts regarding the value of music
catalogs and investment as AI becomes more prevalent.
A: While AI-generated music entering hit charts has been a topic of discussion, its share remains
extremely small at present. Evergreen music catalogs continue to be highly popular as investment
targets, and it is difficult to imagine that they will be able to compete with AI-generated music going
forward. Evergreen catalogs are based on individual experiences and memories, are listened to 
repeatedly over long periods, and sometimes drives behaviors such as attending live performances,
which cannot be substituted by AI. At one point, there was active discussion that DIY music
distribution platforms would eliminate the need for label services, but in reality, this has not occurred,
and we quickly incorporated such platforms into our business while maintaining our label services.
Going forward, rather than merely protecting existing businesses in response to technological
evolution and changes in business models, it is important to proactively capture opportunities.

Q: [Consolidated] Regarding capital allocation, please explain again the execution status of strategic
investments over the past two years and the approach to capital allocation. In addition, please share
key points for the next Mid-Range Plan.
A: For strategic investments, we have set a budget of 1.8 trillion yen in the current Mid-Range Plan,

enhancement of shareholder returns should be understood as being made possible by increased

that perspective? Also, while establishing a JV seems like a good way to improve ROIC, please
